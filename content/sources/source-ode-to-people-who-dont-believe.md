# Chris Williamson, an ode to people who don't believe in themselves

A short Modern Wisdom video by Chris Williamson (YouTube, 6:53) on whether belief or action comes first, with six lessons.

## Concept notes

- [[act-before-you-believe|You don't need self-belief before acting]]. Make it until you believe it, and generate evidence.
- [[chronic-seriousness-makes-you-brittle|Chronic seriousness makes you brittle]]. Joe Hudson's question, what would this be like 10% more enjoyable, and McConaughey on humor as the default emotion.
- [[goals-are-hypotheses|Treat goals as hypotheses to test]]. Goals and attachments are hypotheses to test. They are not ideologies to prove.

## Other points

- Winning should feel like joy as well as the end of fear. How you win and feel during it matter more than whether you win, and how readers are affected matters more than how many.
- You do things for the experience of having done them, so vibes outweigh outcomes and inputs. The felt experience is what you remember.
- Optimizing for how you feel detaches you from the outcome and tends to drive outcomes best. If not, you still enjoyed it.
- Feeling a lot makes life harder, but depth of thought is why you get the outcomes you want.
- You have dealt with everything so far. Williamson asks whether that was grasping and ruminating or whether you are capable. Over a long time horizon, he says, most people get what they deserve.
- Past a certain material comfort you do it for yourself, and your conscience knows when you are honest.
- Be the person your mom thinks you are, or the role model your youngest self wanted. Williamson quotes the ancient Greeks, "live as though all your ancestors were living again through you."
- People are judged by how much life they can tolerate, meaning how much feeling they can carry and enjoy.

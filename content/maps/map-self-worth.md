# Self-worth and identity

Where ambition comes from, what self-worth rests on, and lessons about the self that only land late.

## Ambition

- [[ambition-vs-entitlement|Ambition expects you to close the gap and entitlement expects others to]]. Ambition means expecting yourself to close the gap between what you have and what you want. Entitlement means expecting others to.
- [[ambition-can-be-insecurity|Outsized ambition is often powered by an outsized wound]]. Alain de Botton argues that big achievement and the wish for fame usually compensate for invisibility, a lack of love or an early problem.
- [[envy-is-a-signal|Envy points at capabilities you may have]]. De Botton treats envy as a sign of a possible future self, tested with an exercise and checked against the cost of that life.
- [[make-peace-with-your-averageness|Happiness is achieving what you are capable of]]. Alain de Botton says happiness comes from using your own capacity, and that tolerating averageness in most fields marks the start of wisdom.

## Worth and self-image

- [[self-worth-beyond-validation|Self-worth outsourced to approval is never secure]]. Approval, achievement and comparison are unstable places to rest your worth, and the validation you want is usually permission to validate yourself.
- [[nobody-is-thinking-about-you|Nobody is thinking about you as much as you think]]. Your sense of self runs on imagined judgments from people who are mostly absorbed in themselves, and the harshest critic is internal.
- [[the-true-self-is-chosen|The true self is projected from your values]]. People call a change authentic when it matches their own values, so the true self looks more invented than discovered.
- [[the-inner-citadel|When blocked from what we want, we teach ourselves not to want it]]. Isaiah Berlin's Inner Citadel is the retreat in which people stop wanting what they cannot get, which protects them and also limits them.
- [[vulnerability-is-not-weakness|Restraint is not the same as resilience]]. Hiding what you feel is restraint. Resilience is feeling fully and staying open, which is why vulnerability takes courage.
- [[love-cannot-be-earned|Love that must be earned is not love]]. Many high achievers learned as children that love follows performance, and keep trying to earn it with status, money or fame.

## Choices and lessons

- [[curse-of-competence|Being good at many things makes every choice your fault]]. When you can do many things, your options are limited by your own choices, so every unhappy outcome is yours to answer for.
- [[unteachable-lessons|The most important lessons sound like cliches until you live them]]. Some advice is only learned firsthand, and the most important lessons are the ones that sound like cliches beforehand.
- [[advice-amplifies-what-you-are|Advice amplifies what you already are]]. Advice exaggerates existing traits instead of reshaping people, so those who need it least take the most of it.
- [[mimetic-desire|Most wants are borrowed from other people]]. Mimetic desire is wanting something because someone else wants it or has it, which makes a desire feel personal while being borrowed.

## Humility

- [[the-position-is-not-you|Better treatment from a position belongs to the position]]. As you gain a position, people treat you better. That treatment is for the position and the level you reached, and nobody else.
- [[speak-last|Speak last so people feel heard and you decide with more information]]. Simon Sinek advises speaking last in a group, so everyone feels heard and you act after hearing them.

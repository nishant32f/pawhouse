# Startups, leadership and AI

Ambition in the AI era, running teams, building companies and building wealth.

## AI and ambition

- [[boil-the-ocean|AI is a reason to attempt bigger projects]]. Garry Tan argues that superintelligence should be used to build something 10x better, and running the current plan cheaper falls short of that.
- [[ephemeralization|Doing more with less frees capacity to attempt more]]. Fuller's ephemeralization is doing more with less until you can do everything with nothing, and it argues for using freed capacity.
- [[jevons-paradox|Cheaper use of a resource can raise total demand]]. When a resource becomes much more efficient to use, total consumption can rise, and Tan expects this for intelligence.
- [[ambition-with-ai-models|Use AI models to raise speed, altitude, depth and range]]. Rieseberg splits ambition with models into four dimensions and says higher-level delegation needs data, tools and independent checks.
- [[software-factories|A software factory is defined by who takes the call when production breaks]]. Chamath defines a software factory as an accountable production system, and gives five tests that separate it from an AI coding tool.
- [[code-factory|Agent-written code merges only through deterministic gates]]. Ryan Carson's setup for a repo where agents write and review code and merge depends on auditable, current-commit checks.

## Leading people

- [[executive-responsibilities|An executive charts the course, sets the pace and holds the line]]. DHH splits an executive's core work into charting the course, setting the pace and holding the line on quality.
- [[problem-solving-capacity|Problem-solving capacity must keep up with problem creation]]. Kalanick says a company's ambition is limited by management capacity, so problems should be created no faster than they can be solved.
- [[four-beliefs-behind-motivation|Low effort often means a missing belief]]. Duckworth's four thoughts behind sustained effort, used as a conversation checklist before treating low effort as a character problem.
- [[hiring|Hire for traits and treat a split decision as a no]]. Seven traits to look for in candidates, how long to interview, when to decide and what not to ask.
- [[the-quiet-ones|Reviews reward visible work over essential invisible work]]. Performance matrices miss people whose work crosses levels and prevents problems, and those people leave when they stop expecting to be seen.
- [[speak-last|Speak last so people feel heard and you decide with more information]]. Simon Sinek advises speaking last in a group, so everyone feels heard and you act after hearing them.
- [[the-position-is-not-you|Better treatment from a position belongs to the position]]. As you gain a position, people treat you better. That treatment is for the position and the level you reached, and nobody else.
- [[resignation-retention-letter|Resignation retention letter]]. A letter pattern for responding to a valued teammate's resignation with warmth and a real invitation to stay.

## Building companies

- [[startup-operating-principles|Startup operating principles]]. Principles compiled from older notes on business imperatives, product management, money and scaling.
- [[ideation|Good startup ideas come from living in the future]]. Start from users who urgently need something, live in the future and build what is missing, and test the problem against five criteria.
- [[startup-blindness|Ignorance of obstacles is what lets founders start]]. Ignorance is the best antidote to schlep blindness, and asking what problem you wish someone would solve for you finds the rest.
- [[excellence-is-capacity-to-take-pain|Excellence is the capacity to take pain]]. Pain tolerance sets how large a problem you can take on, but numbness and fragility are both failure modes.

## Career and wealth

- [[wealth-building-rules|Hormozi's rules for building wealth]]. Rules for building wealth in your 20s and 30s from Alex Hormozi, who built companies with $100M+ in annual revenue.
- [[late-stage-success-advice|Successful people give advice from the maintenance phase]]. Successful people often describe the strategies they use now, which differ from the ones that built their success.
- [[operator-to-strategist|Moving from operator to strategist means giving up busyness as identity]]. The shift from grinding operator to strategic thinker is hard because busyness has become proof of success.
- [[curse-of-competence|Being good at many things makes every choice your fault]]. When you can do many things, your options are limited by your own choices, so every unhappy outcome is yours to answer for.

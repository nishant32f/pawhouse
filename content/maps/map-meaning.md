# Meaning and presence

Why reaching goals disappoints, how time gets spent, and what meaning is made of.

## Living for later

- [[arrival-fallacy|Reaching a goal does not deliver the state you expected]]. The arrival fallacy is the belief that reaching a milestone will bring peace, worth or security. The mind adjusts, and the goal stops satisfying.
- [[you-may-already-have-the-life|You may already have the life you are working to earn]]. A parable about a plan whose end point is the life the fisherman already has, and a short note on what money is for.
- [[postponed-living|Postponing life to buy time later makes the postponement permanent]]. Trading time for money and happiness for success to enjoy life later builds a habit of postponement that outlasts the trade.
- [[frankls-inverse-law|People who can't feel pleasure hide in meaning and delayed gratification]]. The inverse of Frankl's principle. Those who struggle to feel joy chase hard things forever and never collect the reward.
- [[busyness-as-anesthesia|Busyness and achievement can numb what you would otherwise feel]]. Busyness keeps you moving too fast to feel loneliness or misalignment. Remove it and the feelings remain, which is a first honest contact.
- [[operator-to-strategist|Moving from operator to strategist means giving up busyness as identity]]. The shift from grinding operator to strategic thinker is hard because busyness has become proof of success.

## Time and attention

- [[parkinsons-law|Work expands to fill the time available]]. Parkinson's Law explains missing deadlines and untouched cupboards. A related effect lets compulsive phone use take two hours a day.
- [[novelty-lengthens-remembered-time|Novelty lengthens remembered time and routine shrinks it]]. Time passes at one speed while it happens. How long it feels afterwards depends on how many distinct memories it left.
- [[avoiding-boredom-empties-life|Avoiding boredom with screens makes life feel emptier]]. Boredom feels bad, so people distract themselves, which lowers tolerance for boredom. Boundaries and sitting with yourself break the loop.
- [[judge-content-by-how-you-feel-after|Judge content by how you feel after consuming it]]. Compelling content is not necessarily good for you, and the way to tell is to check how you feel once it ends.

## What meaning is

- [[you-cant-simulate-meaning|You can't simulate meaning]]. Algorithmic life offers near substitutes for connection, status and purpose. They ease the surface of a need and leave the need unmet.
- [[meaning-is-coherence-purpose-significance|Meaning has three parts: coherence, purpose, significance]]. Michael Steger's three components of meaning, with Brooks's account of what supplies each and how meaning tends to arrive.
- [[happiness-has-three-parts|Happiness needs enjoyment, satisfaction and meaning]]. Brooks defines happiness as three macronutrients. Strivers tend to have plenty of satisfaction, little enjoyment and too little meaning.
- [[complex-problems-need-different-tools|Complex life questions can't be solved with tools for complicated problems]]. Analysis and optimisation solve mechanical problems. Love, a calling and a marriage are lived mysteries and need another mode of attention.
- [[love-cannot-be-earned|Love that must be earned is not love]]. Many high achievers learned as children that love follows performance, and keep trying to earn it with status, money or fame.
- [[strengths-and-wounds-are-one-metal|Your strengths and your wounds come from the same source]]. A trait that makes someone effective at work may damage intimacy. People blame parents for flaws and do not credit them for strengths.
- [[meaningful-vs-pointless-suffering|Suffering helps only when it builds capacity or serves meaning]]. Hardship earns its place when it develops capacity or serves a value you endorse, and pointless loyalty to pain does neither.

## Holding life lightly

- [[chronic-seriousness-makes-you-brittle|Chronic seriousness makes you brittle]]. Seriousness gives an edge in bursts but makes long-term persistence rigid. Joy is what winning should feel like, rather than relief.
- [[optimised-leisure-becomes-work|Optimising rest turns leisure into work]]. Meditation, hobbies and friendships stop restoring you when each becomes a contest to win or an activity to improve.
- [[appreciate-the-ordinary|Appreciation of ordinary things is a practice]]. Warmth, smiles, effort and being here are easy to overlook. Appreciation is something you do, and it covers lessons as well as blessings.
- [[aliveness-erodes-by-small-bargains|Aliveness fades through small compromises and returns through small acts]]. The loss the quote points to is the slow numbing of curiosity, tenderness and hope. One small act can start to restore it.
- [[the-shower-test|Where your mind goes in the shower shows what you care about]]. With nothing pulling on your attention, the mind returns to what you actually care about. It also shows whether a desire is yours.

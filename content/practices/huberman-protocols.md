# Huberman protocols

Andrew Huberman is a neuroscientist and tenured professor in the Department of Neurobiology at Stanford University School of Medicine. He hosts the Huberman Lab podcast and wrote the book "Protocols: An Operating Manual for the Human Body" (2024).

The list below is a community summary of his lectures, written by its poster for personal use. It keeps the practical protocols and drops the ones aimed at excessive optimising. Topics not covered, such as testosterone, brain chemistry or the immune system, come down to a healthy diet, good sleep and regular exercise.

## Sleep

| Time or action | Protocol | Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Upon waking | 5-10 min sunlight (even if cloudy) | #1 most important | Sets circadian clock |
| 10pm-4am | Avoid bright lights, especially overhead | #2 most important | Red or firelight is fine, low lights are less harmful |
| 8-10 hrs before bed | Stop caffeine | High | Disrupts sleep |
| Near bedtime | Avoid alcohol | Medium | Disrupts sleep quality |
| Sleep environment | Cool and dark | High | Optimal conditions |
| Schedule | Limit variation | High | Consistency matters more than total hours |
| Naps | Under 90 minutes | OK | Will not disrupt the cycle |
| Jet lag | Shift clock gradually | As needed | Plan ahead |

Supplements matter less than these behaviours.
- Apigenin (chamomile tea) or theanine if you have trouble falling asleep.
- NSDR for insomnia.
- Melatonin is not recommended by Huberman.
- Magnesium has limited evidence except in niche cases.

## Caffeine

- Take no more than 1-3 mg per kg of bodyweight to avoid discomfort.
- If caffeine makes you crash in the afternoon, delay it 90-120 minutes after waking.
- It reinforces whatever you consume or do alongside it, so you like that thing more.
- Tablets are an alternative to caffeinated drinks.
- Releases acetylcholine (alertness) and dopamine (mood and motivation).
- Improves reaction time and recall.
- Blocks adenosine receptors, which inhibits sleepiness.
- Increases the dopaminergic effect of exercise and the desire for it.

## Alcohol

- Less alcohol means fewer health risks.
- An average of 1-2 drinks per day across a week is associated with thinning of the neocortex, more impulsivity, higher baseline cortisol, lower mood, higher cancer risk and lower testosterone.
- Electrolytes before drinking can reduce hangover severity.
- Spiking adrenaline, for example with cold exposure, helps a hangover.

## Cannabis

- Sativa acts as a stimulant, raising creativity, energy and focus and reducing pain.
- Indica has a fuller body effect and promotes relaxation and relief from anxiety.
- Hybrids blend the two.

Potential adverse effects:
- Use before age 25 affects brain development in proportion to amount used. It thins gray matter, particularly in the frontal lobe, with long-term effects on executive function. It raises the risk of depression or anxiety, and of psychosis in those genetically predisposed.
- All the risks of smoking or vaping anything.
- May raise baseline anxiety in chronic users.
- Depression has been found to rise with chronic use, especially in people who were not depressed when they started.

## Cold exposure

- A cold shower works and an ice bath is better.
- Make it cold enough that you would like to get out but can stay in safely, for about 1-5 minutes.
- A hot shower afterwards keeps the benefits, minus the metabolic increase.
- The body heats up afterwards, which can make falling asleep harder.
- Spikes epinephrine and norepinephrine (focus and energy) and sustains a rise in dopamine (mood and motivation).
- Works as resilience training.
- Over time raises metabolism and converts white fat to beige and brown fat through shivering.
- After exercise it improves recovery and reduces soreness for people whose goal is recovery rather than adaptation, such as before a competition.
- Cold exposure or anti-inflammatory drugs within 4 hours after exercise may inhibit muscle gain.

## Heat exposure

- Use a sauna for 5-20 minutes per session.
- One session a week is good, 2-3 ideal, 7 the maximum.
- Drink about 6-18 oz of water for every 20 minutes.
- Benefits include better cardiovascular health, a slower age-related decline in growth hormone, better mood and maintained protein structures.

## Diet

- Aim for 25 g fiber per day and 500-600 g of fruit and vegetables per day.
- Eat a balanced diet of plenty of whole foods, fruit and vegetables, whole grains, healthy fats (nuts, seeds, fatty fish), enough protein and dairy.
- Whole foods are high in nutrients and tend to lower calorie intake if that is your goal.
- Refined sugar is high in calories and low in nutrients. Chronic overconsumption is associated with higher risk of type 2 diabetes, heart disease and certain cancers. It causes tooth decay and creates cravings for more sugar.
- People who abstain completely from something such as sugar are more likely to binge on it.

## Gut microbiome

- Matters for mental and physical health.
- Eat fiber and low-sugar fermented foods, avoid too much processed food, and sleep well.

## Water

- Filter tap water, preferably with a filter that removes fluoride.
- Hard water (high in calcium and magnesium) can reduce inflammation and benefit cardiovascular health.
- Drink about 80 oz within 10 hours of waking.
- Avoid plastic water bottles, since plastic leaches into the water over time.
- Electrolytes help offset dehydration and suit exercise.

## Fitness

- Use a weightlifting program from Liftvault or the r/fitness wiki rather than complex lectures.
- Keep weight sessions under 60 minutes, 75 at most, to avoid excess cortisol and keep recovery sound.
- After a workout, do 3-5 minutes of downregulation breathing. Slow deliberate breathing, physiological sighs or any pattern that emphasises exhales activates the parasympathetic system and lowers stress hormones.
- Muscle gain needs 0.6-1 g protein per lb of bodyweight per day. Bulking is not required. At a healthy body fat you can eat at maintenance with enough protein while training.
- Weight loss needs a calorie deficit (see the r/loseit wiki). Diet matters more than activity, though over 70% of people who lose weight and keep it off exercise regularly.
- Do endurance cardio and HIIT, generally 2-4 cardio sessions per week.
- Heat impedes performance. Cool your AVAs (palms, soles of the feet, upper face). Evidence for performance gains from cooling is weak.
- Supplements to consider are creatine, caffeine and rhodiola rosea.

## Flexibility

- Microstretching works better than intense stretching. Stretch without much pain, since consistency and frequency matter more than intensity.
- Static stretching is a general routine for range of motion and for recovery and soreness after exercise.
- PNF stretching increases flexibility in targeted muscles for athletics, often with a partner.
- Antagonistic muscle group stretching reduces injury risk before exercise, for gymnastics, dance and martial arts.
- Benefits include slower age-related loss of flexibility, better balance and posture, less pain and inflammation, smoother gait and better athletic performance.

## Focus

- Focus means bringing attention back when it drifts. The average adult sustains unbroken attention for no more than 3 minutes, so do not expect uniform focus.
- Keep work sessions to 90 minutes or less.
- Stare at one spot for 60 seconds to raise focus, since mental focus follows visual focus.
- Your best cognitive work happens within 8 hours of waking.
- Keep your screen at or above eye level to promote alertness.
- Try background sounds such as white, pink or brown noise or binaural beats.

## Learning information

- Keep learning bouts to 90 minutes or less.
- An adrenaline spike after learning, for example from cold exposure, may improve retention. NSDR after learning may also help.
- Use active recall and spaced repetition, and get good sleep.

## Learning skills

- Be alert and focused, and repeat the action you want to improve.
- Expect and embrace errors. The 85% rule says failing about 15% of the time is the best difficulty for skill learning.
- Take a break of about 10 seconds at random every few minutes, which speeds learning.
- Practise consistently, limit sessions to 90 minutes or less, use NSDR afterwards, and sleep well.

## Meditation (NSDR)

- The physiological sigh lowers stress by raising parasympathetic activity. Take a full inhale through the nose, then a second inhale through the nose, then a complete exhale through the mouth, repeated as needed.
- Consider transcendental meditation once or twice a day, and try other forms.
- Benefits include less stress and anxiety, better focus, mood and emotion regulation (more gray matter in related areas) and a stronger prefrontal cortex.

## Motivation

- Dopamine is better described as the molecule of craving and motivation than of pleasure. Motivation follows your current dopamine baseline, which drops after any large spike until it replenishes.
- Use random intermittent reinforcement, the most effective reward schedule for motivation. Celebrate wins, but not all of them.
- Emphasise the process, since mindset partly controls dopamine release.
- Do not layer too many dopamine sources if you want steady motivation, because you will crash afterwards.
- Sleep well.

## Goals

- Visualise the big win to get started and the consequences of failure to stay motivated.
- Make goals moderately lofty, achievable but not easy.
- Set 1-3 major goals per year.
- Define the end goal and the steps to it concretely.
- Assess progress regularly, for example weekly.

## Longevity

- Exercise regularly, sleep well and eat a healthy diet.
- Maintain muscle mass for functional aging.
- Minimise chronic inflammation, including chronic stress.
- NAD+ and resveratrol may increase longevity, but the research is unclear.

## Supplements

Stronger evidence exists for these.
- Ashwagandha reduces cortisol.
- Caffeine.
- Creatine improves physical performance and possibly cognition. It is among the best researched and must be taken daily.
- Omega-3 fatty acids can reduce depression and improve cognition. Aim for products with at least 1 g of EPA plus DHA. Doses as high as 2 g of EPA have shown benefit.
- Rhodiola rosea reduces stress and improves performance under stress, work capacity and recovery from intense workouts.

For a specific gap, such as magnesium or vitamin D, take something for that. Consider a multivitamin if you decide you need one.

## Closing thoughts

The poster's closing advice is not to lose sight of the overall aim. The protocols exist to improve quality of life. Optimising for its own sake is no goal. Nothing is optimal in every way, and what is ideal in one way is less ideal in another.

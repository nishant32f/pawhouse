# Resignation retention letter

A letter pattern for when a strong teammate resigns and the honest response is to fight for them respectfully rather than accept it passively. The source is a user-provided screenshot.

## Use it when

- A high-agency person resigns for an external opportunity.
- You believe they are central to what is being built.
- The relationship has enough trust for emotional honesty.
- You want to show conviction without guilt-tripping them.
- You want retention to rest on meaning, people and a shared future as well as compensation.

## The letter in the screenshot

Subject: **Resignation / Keanu Reeves**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I spent a few days thinking about your resignation and somehow ended up with a quote from Keanu Reeves: "If you're a lover, you gotta be a fighter... because if you don't fight for your love, what kind of love do you have?" He reframes the idea of "fighting" not as conflict, but as effort, showing up, protecting what matters, and choosing love even when it is inconvenient or uncomfortable.
>
> I happen to love working with great people that are eager and unafraid of creating the future, so quintessentially you.
>
> So, although I am happy for you for your external opportunity, I have real difficulty restraining my urge to fight. Especially since I feel that we are at the cusp of something truly great that I would...

The screenshot ends there.

## Why it works

1. It honors the decision. It does not open by attacking the resignation or dismissing the other opportunity.
2. It names the person's identity ("great people... eager and unafraid of creating the future") as well as their output.
3. It frames the fight as effort, commitment and protection of what matters, without coercion.
4. It contrasts the outside opportunity with a mission described as rare and unfinished.

## Adaptable template

Hi [Name],

I spent a few days thinking about your resignation and kept coming back to the idea that when something matters, you show up for it and do not let it pass quietly. You fight for it through effort, honesty, and a willingness to say what is true, without pressure or conflict.

I genuinely love working with people who are eager, thoughtful, and unafraid of building the future. That is how I see you.

So although I am happy for you and respect the opportunity in front of you, I also have a hard time pretending I do not want to fight for you to stay. I believe we are at the cusp of something important, and I believe you are one of the people who can help shape it.

I do not want to guilt you or make this decision harder in an unfair way. But I would regret not telling you clearly: I value you, I believe in what we are building together, and I would love to explore whether there is a version of the future here that still feels exciting enough for you to choose.

If you are open to it, I would like to talk before you make this final.

## Tone notes

- Keep it personal and out of corporate language.
- Do not make the person responsible for your feelings.
- Do not imply betrayal.
- Do not focus on money unless compensation is actually the issue.
- Make a real ask, such as one more conversation, one revised role or one clearer future.
- Send it only if you mean it. The letter is personal, so a retention attempt you do not mean will read as false.

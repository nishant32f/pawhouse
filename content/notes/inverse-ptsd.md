# Surviving a hard period raises what you believe you can handle

Joe Rogan: "The worst thing that's ever happened to you is the worst thing that's ever happened to you." The saddest you have been is the saddest you have been, and the hardest you have worked is the hardest you have worked.

Hard times are not fun while they happen, for the same reason nobody thinks they are living through a golden era. Golden years only exist in the past, because only in hindsight do we see our worries were a waste of time. While you are inside it, every concern is an open loop and something to fear. Looking back, you see you could handle them.

If you have just come out of the hardest period of your life, that is a reason to celebrate. You can now handle a higher workload. Each challenge is something you feared and survived, and each limit you break tells you that you can handle more than before.

Chris calls this inverse PTSD, a kind of workload exposure therapy that teaches "Oh I've been here before and I didn't die, this is ok."

The 1100th Modern Wisdom episode repeats the point and adds that a hard life does not always need to become easier. Sometimes it needs to become simpler. Many small open loops make you feel crushed, and simplicity lets you do things in order and make progress.

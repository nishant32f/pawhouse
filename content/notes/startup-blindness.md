# Ignorance of obstacles is what lets founders start

Schlep blindness is not seeing a good idea because it involves unpleasant work. Paul Graham says the most valuable antidote is probably ignorance. Most successful founders would say they might never have started had they known the obstacles ahead. That may be one reason the most successful startups so often have young founders.

Founders grow with the problems, but nobody can foresee that, including older and more experienced founders. Young founders make two mistakes that cancel out. They do not know how much they can grow, and they do not know how much they will need to. Older founders make only the first.

Ignorance cannot solve everything. Some ideas obviously involve alarming schleps that anyone can see. To find those, take yourself out of the picture. Instead of asking what problem you should solve, ask what problem you wish someone else would solve for you. Someone who had to process payments before Stripe, asking that, would have wished for Stripe early. It is too late to be Stripe, but plenty is still broken for those who can see it.

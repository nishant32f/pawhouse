# Judge content by how you feel after consuming it

While you consume something you are absorbed in it, so you cannot judge what it does to you. The judgment has to come afterwards. Chris Williamson calls this post content clarity.

Creators design content to hold attention, because one who does not is beaten by one who does. Compelling does not mean good for you. People hate-watch adversarial videos to see their side make the other side look silly, pity-follow accounts to watch a catastrophe in someone's life, and browse Twitter arguments with a racing heart and unspoken indignation. Then they forget they consumed it and never assess whether it was good for them. Attention goes to whoever is boldest and most aggressive, and the next day it happens again.

## The check

Ask how different creators make you feel afterwards.

- Some channels hold you but leave you tense, negative, cynical and thinking in zero-sum terms. You do not want to message friends, compliment anyone or go outside, and the world feels against you.
- Other content leaves you connected, hopeful, open, prepared, informed, light and aligned.

Choose by the second, however much the first fires dopamine. Just as the body is built from what you eat, the mind is built from what you see and hear.

Freedom requires thinking for yourself. Without living intentionally you end up following base instincts, the worst norms around you and confused chemical signals, even if you are rich, successful or famous. You spend life on aims others or your worst parts defined, pass those assumptions to children and loved ones, and reinforce the same defaults in everyone you meet.

# Obsession removes the friction from hard work

Chris Williamson separates three engines of action by how they treat friction.

- Discipline is "I will make myself do the thing." It accepts friction.
- Motivation is "I want to do the thing." It reduces friction.
- Obsession is "I can't not do the thing." It inverts friction.

In his words, "Discipline is friction accepted. Motivation is friction reduced. Obsession is friction inverted."

From outside, obsession looks like superhuman discipline. From inside it can feel almost unavoidable. It cannot be summoned on command. It appears when curiosity, identity, reward and meaning happen to line up, and it eventually fades. Williamson calls it "a nonrenewable fuel source."

## What to do with it

If a positive obsession appears, do not dilute it early in the name of balance. Use it while it lasts, and let it build the habits, skills, routines and self-image that remain after it cools.

This also changes how to read discipline. "What often looks like discipline today is just the echo of someone's past obsessions." What once obsessed him has become part of him, so discipline can be the residue of an earlier obsession rather than a starting point.

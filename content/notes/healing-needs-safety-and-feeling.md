# Healing needs safety plus contact with emotion

Chris Williamson reduces many psychological healing methods to two moves:

1. Create a container safe enough for the mind to become more malleable.
2. Experience difficult emotions inside that container until old mental habits update.

Suppression therefore does not count as strength. Development comes from work, and age alone does not supply it. A feeling that was never safely met has not necessarily been processed. It may only be stored. This is also why [[overthinking-is-underfeeling|Overthinking is often underfeeling]].

## Detachment

> "Life is always going to feel distant if you're detached."

If you follow the creed of ignoring your feelings and working harder, achievement can arrive without felt aliveness. Numbing pain also numbs reward, and the armour that protects you from discomfort can keep you from the life you were building. See [[busyness-as-anesthesia|Busyness and achievement can numb what you would otherwise feel]].

## Resentment

Resentment often works as an unskilled guard. It tries to stop the same pain from happening again and may keep reacting after the danger has passed.

> "Resentment is more likely to ease up if you say 'thank you' than 'fuck you'."

That does not mean obeying it. It means understanding what it is protecting before asking it to stand down.

## Sadness and anger

> "Sadness at home gets sympathy. Anger at home gets disciplined. Is it any surprise we so often turn anger into sadness?"

Emotions learn which forms are safe to express. If anger was punished and sadness comforted, anger may turn itself into sadness before it reaches awareness.

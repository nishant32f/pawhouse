# Wanting things to be different blocks happiness

Naval Ravikant: "Happiness is the state when nothing is missing. When nothing is missing, your mind shuts down and stops running into the past or future to regret something or to plan something."

If you want the world to be different, your happiness is held until that change happens. Sometimes the change is one you should make yourself, such as leaving an unhappy friendship, changing from an unfulfilling career, or having a difficult conversation. The wanting is then a signal to act.

People often stay in years of misery to avoid a few minutes of pain (see [[region-beta-paradox|Tolerable situations keep you stuck longer than bad ones]]).

The other roadblock to happiness is uncertainty, covered in [[the-brain-prefers-catastrophe-to-uncertainty|The brain prefers an imagined catastrophe to not knowing]]. The two can overlap, and when you feel unhappy it helps to look at both first.

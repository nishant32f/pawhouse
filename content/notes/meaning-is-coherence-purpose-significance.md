# Meaning has three parts: coherence, purpose, significance

Arthur Brooks uses Michael Steger's three components of meaning.

1. **Coherence.** Life makes enough sense to act inside it, via religion, science, philosophy or conspiracy theories. Brooks reads deep conspiracy belief as a distorted cry for coherence, so throwing facts at it may miss the meaning crisis underneath.
2. **Purpose.** A direction in which you can make progress. Goals need not be finishable. Brooks prefers becoming a better father, husband, friend or citizen, or a creator of value, over goals you complete and drop.
3. **Significance.** Your life matters to someone or something beyond you. Fame is attention. Significance is mattering to someone. A dog, spouse, child, friend, community or God can make you significant in a way a follower count cannot. Brooks jokes about a show called *I Used to Be Famous*, since culture likes zero-to-hero stories and avoids hero-to-zero ones.

## How meaning returns

- **Transcendence.** Mother Nature keeps us in the "me self" of work stress, money, status and logistics. Awe, service, prayer and love move attention outward to the "I self". Brooks recommends service and prayer even to the nonreligious and says "you don't find meaning; meaning finds you."
- **Calling.** Not fun, glamorous or world-saving. It is what you cannot stop thinking about, and it needs real value and someone who needs you. If it is highly rewarding and you are very unhappy, it is probably not your calling.
- **Beauty.** Artistic, moral (kindness for no apparent reason) and natural (real nature rather than a screen saver). Too little means you are probably too far left in the brain.
- **Suffering.** A major source of meaning. Brooks does not praise pain. We must suffer, and the task is to turn it into meaning instead of numbing it. See [[meaningful-vs-pointless-suffering|Suffering helps only when it builds capacity or serves meaning]].

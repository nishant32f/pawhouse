# Being ashamed of small fears makes them worse

We inherited a nervous system calibrated for lions and use it for awkward conversations and underwhelming careers. Ancestors needed courage to keep their bodies alive. We need it to keep our identity intact. Old dangers could kill the body and new ones threaten belonging, but the limbic system evolved for a world that is gone. It reacts the same way to a bear or a boundary, as if telling the truth could mean dying alone in the wilderness.

Suffering gets worse when shame about the fear arrives. A voice says other people had it much worse. Knowing your life is not collapsing does not slow your racing heart and only adds guilt. The belief that a comfortable life makes panic ridiculous is the shame of small fears. It makes your emotions pass a severity threshold before you may feel them, and shaming yourself for an emotion adds a second wound. Be gentle with yourself when normal things scare you, and do not shame yourself for the shaming either.

## Modern bravery

Modern courage is smaller because the stakes are rarely life or death, and harder because the threats are invisible. You cannot swing an axe at uncertainty or outrun heartbreak. The acts are small and private:

- telling the truth
- saying no
- walking away from a career that looks good on paper but feels wrong
- letting a friend down instead of letting yourself down
- admitting you want more than other people are used to

There is no applause or medal, yet they shape a life. Bravery is knowing the world will not end if you speak up, feeling your stomach drop anyway, and doing it.

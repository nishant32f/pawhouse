# Preparation becomes avoidance once it delays execution

You do not need to feel motivated, resolved, healed, certain or prepared to act. Preparation is useful only while it increases execution. Once it delays execution, it is avoidance.

## Motivation and discipline

You can act without feeling motivated, and acting is what counts. Discipline beats mood because it does not depend on wanting to train, write, apologize, apply or ship. Interest feels good on easy days and commitment keeps moving on hard ones. Interest reads the book. Commitment applies one lesson, starting today and continuing after the feeling has passed.

## Perfectionism and strategy

Perfectionism can pass as quality control while protecting you from the judgment of the real world. Useful standards improve the work and neurotic ones stop the work from being released. Ask whether a change improves the result or helps you avoid the discomfort of releasing, failing or being seen. Strategy is mentally clean and lets you feel smart without risking proof. Execution is physically and emotionally expensive and makes contact with reality.

## Procrastination as fear of being seen failing

Procrastination is often about self-worth more than time management. If you try and fail, everyone sees. If you never try, the failure stays private and you keep the story that you could have done it. Ask what you are afraid will be true about you if you actually try. The antidote is accepting being seen as a beginner, which means risking your image instead of protecting it.

Victor Hugo finished The Hunchback of Notre Dame by locking away all his clothes except a wool shawl, buying a large bottle of ink and staying indoors until the book was done.

When stuck, shrink the problem until the next real-world action is obvious, and do it before thinking more.

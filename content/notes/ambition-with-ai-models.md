# Use AI models to raise speed, altitude, depth and range

Felix Rieseberg, in "How to be more ambitious: A tutorial for humans with models" (13 October 2026), separates ambition into four dimensions.

- **Speed.** Complete familiar work faster.
- **Altitude.** Move from producing an artifact to achieving its purpose. A quarterly deck becomes a system for detecting problems and improving decisions.
- **Depth.** Apply expert methods to raw evidence. Test assumptions, look for new signals, and validate against something beyond the model's own judgment.
- **Range.** Borrow techniques from other disciplines. Seek their investigative methods and skip theatrical role-play.

Higher-level delegation needs infrastructure. The model needs access to relevant data, software and environments for testing. In his crash-fixing example, that means connecting reports to code versions and reproducing failures across platforms.

## Startup application

The following is an application of the framework and is no quotation from the article. Before delegating a project, write down:

1. The business outcome and the decision it should enable.
2. The evidence the agent may access and the boundaries it must respect.
3. The methods worth testing, including those outside your usual discipline.
4. An acceptance test that can fail independently of a persuasive explanation.

Start with a reversible trial. Broader ambition should raise the quality of the experiment and leave accountability in place.

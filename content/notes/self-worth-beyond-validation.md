# Self-worth outsourced to approval is never secure

What you want from validation is usually permission to validate yourself. Approval won by compromising yourself does not satisfy you, because part of you knows the cost.

Other people's reactions are too unstable to hold an identity. Moods change, values differ, attention is limited, approval is conditional, and criticism may say more about them than about you. Use feedback as information. Do not make it the basis of your worth.

**Neediness** is ranking what others think of you above what you think of yourself. Each time you show up as someone else to please another person, you reject yourself.

**Persona and person.** A false identity softens criticism and praise alike, since both are one step removed. A persona can receive praise but not love. Being yourself does not make everyone like you. It lets the right people know you. Chris Williamson adds, "As you bring more awareness to yourself, it becomes harder to not be yourself," since self-awareness makes small lies, value differences and unsaid truths more abrasive.

**Who versus what.** Most people want to be loved for who they are, yet many only love themselves when they perform. That asks unconditional regard from others while offering yourself conditional acceptance. See [[love-cannot-be-earned|Love that must be earned is not love]].

**Comparison.** People want to feel happier than others as well as to be happy. You see your own confusion from the inside and other people's highlights. Count a life's private cost before envying it, as in [[envy-is-a-signal|Envy points at capabilities you may have]].

**Standards.** Self-love can include high standards. Contempt says "be better because you are worthless." Self-respect says "be better because you are worth the effort."

Before chasing approval, ask whether you are sacrificing self-worth, the thing you want, for validation, the thing you hope will get it.

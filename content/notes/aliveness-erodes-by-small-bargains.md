# Aliveness fades through small compromises and returns through small acts

> "The tragedy of life is not death, but what we let die inside of us while we live."

The quote is widely attributed to Norman Cousins, with variants in wording. This version comes from an Instagram reel by @invictuskhan_. Death is inevitable, but inner deadness is not. The loss the quote describes is the daily erosion of aliveness, and the final event is secondary. Most people do not lose themselves at once. They make small bargains with disappointment, fear, respectability, busyness, cynicism and fatigue.

A functional life is not automatically a living one. You can be productive, respected and safe while becoming less available to joy, and the world rewards reliability, competence and composure. Enduring without renewal makes a person less present over time.

## What tends to die first

- Curiosity, when efficiency replaces wonder.
- Play, when adulthood becomes performance and responsibility.
- Tenderness, when pain turns into guardedness.
- Ambition, when repeated disappointment passes as realism.
- Hope, when the future stops feeling worth joining.
- Self-respect, when survival takes too many small betrayals of yourself.
- Love, when protection becomes isolation.

## Repair

Bring one deadened part back into use, enough to show it is still reachable. Read something that wakes you up. Call someone without needing a reason. Make something badly but honestly. Say the truer sentence. Take a walk without optimising it. Admit what you still want. Notice where cynicism protects grief. The aim is to stop going along with your own dimming.

Ask regularly what part of you has gone quiet because it was inconvenient to keep alive, then do one small thing that feeds it.

# You can be anything but not everything

Chris Williamson recalls the line "You can be anything you want to be, but you can't be everything you want to be."

## You can be anything

People think they can change small things (a haircut, drinking less, a gym routine) but not start a new career at 40, leave an unhappy long relationship or move country. Far more is within reach. Limiting beliefs are held by nearly everyone, so breaking them puts you in a very small group with a large advantage.

## You cannot be everything

People overestimate how many goals they can juggle. Time is finite and goals contradict each other. You cannot find a partner by going out three times a week while working for a promotion, saving as much as possible and getting in the best shape of your life. You can make progress on one thing in the medium term or on nothing meaningful in the long term. Gains go to those who go narrow and deep while most go shallow and wide. At equal skill, one focus makes twice the progress of two. Progress needs saying no, choosing your highest points of contribution and deciding in advance what you will be bad at.

## Focus is what you say no to

Alex Hormozi says the most focused person says yes to one thing and no to everything else, and focus is measured by the opportunities you reject. 100x results need 100x focus, and slightly better tactics fall short. He had nine businesses with lots of revenue and zero profit, and cutting to one changed the result. Every hour not spent on your one thing goes to something less important than your stated goal, and your actions show your real priorities.

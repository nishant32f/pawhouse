# You can't simulate meaning

Arthur Brooks says modern life feels unreal because much of it is simulation. He does not mean a literal Matrix. He means an algorithmic environment that gives enough stimulation, novelty and pseudo-connection to prevent boredom while taking attention, energy and money. People mostly lack meaning, even with comfort, money and success.

Substitutes ease the surface and leave the need unmet.
- Scrolling for attention.
- Online status for significance, approval for love.
- Dating apps for embodied courtship.
- Gaming achievements and productivity dashboards for purpose.
- Pornography or AI companions for intimacy. Pornography may ease loneliness briefly and tends to increase it.
- Virtual friends for embodied friendship. Chris Williamson found that a 30-minute coffee made an online friend real in three dimensions.

Brains evolved in small kin groups where face-to-face relationships were needed for survival, and eye contact releases oxytocin. Zoom, text and feeds lack that signal. In Brooks's marriage retreats, couples lie in bed and look into each other's eyes for five minutes.

## A life written in disappearing ink

Brooks describes a maximally meaningless day. You wake late to a phone alarm, check it in bed, eat processed food while scrolling, take caffeine at once and spend the first hour programmed by screens. You work from the bedroom among Zoom squares, date through two-dimensional profiles from the same bed, and spend the evening scrolling, gaming and avoiding commitments. He calls this writing your life in disappearing ink.

## Return to reality

The remedy is contact with what cannot be fully optimised. That means face-to-face love, silence and boredom, beauty, service, commitment, real friendship, chosen responsibility, suffering turned into compassion or courage, and work that would matter without applause. More stimulation usually does not help. Ask:
- Where have I replaced contact with proxies?
- What am I measuring because I am afraid to ask what matters?
- Which simulated reward leaves me emptier afterwards?
- What would still feel meaningful if nobody could see it?

# Being good at many things makes every choice your fault

If you are competent, you can try new things, you do not do badly at them, and you progress faster than most people. Your life direction is then limited by your choices more than by your abilities. People tell you how fortunate you are while you face a problem of its own kind.

Barry Schwartz's *The Paradox of Choice* uses jeans. In 1960 a store had one type, colour and cut. You found your waist size, paid and left. Utility may not have been maximised, but you were satisfied, knowing you got the best available. A bad pair was the store's fault.

In 2024 there is skinny, bootcut, straight, cropped, ripped, blue, grey and acid wash. You can find exactly what you want, and any shortfall is your fault because you did the research. Fewer options made the decision easier.

Skills work the same way. Someone good at one category may be unhappy about what they cannot do, but the constraint narrows the choice. Someone good at many things has many open paths, which is freeing and can also leave them scared, confused and frozen. In one world options are constrained by opportunity. In another they are constrained by discernment.

There is a second cost. You may feel guilty for seeming ungrateful, since the world looks open to you.

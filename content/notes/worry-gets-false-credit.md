# Worry takes credit for results it did not produce

An insecure overachiever worries and obsesses in every pursuit. Because worry shows up everywhere, every success looks like proof that worry enhances performance, and every failure looks like proof that more worry was needed. The belief cannot be falsified. Andrew Wilkinson calls it "a walking anxiety disorder harnessed for productivity."

The link between worry and performance is assumed and has not been observed. After enough years the results mostly come from habit, skill and experience. Fear may have been useful early, to narrow focus and start the obsession. Later the worry continues at the same intensity as at the start. Even people who reach black belt level and trust their capacity often find the worry has left without enthusiasm taking its place. What remains is high expectation.

Worry can also become identity. Peter Crone asks "Who could you be in the absence of your concerns?" If worry is your proof of seriousness, letting it go feels irresponsible instead of free.

The same pattern spreads to rest. Insecure overachievers bring full vigilance to areas that need little of it, including breathwork, meditation and hobbies, which turn into things to optimise and win (see [[optimised-leisure-becomes-work|Optimising rest turns leisure into work]]). Oliver Burkeman's line fits here.

> "Pour your finite time and energy into something infinitely more absorbing than trying to keep life under control, which is actually living it."

Chris's proposal is to assume things will go well. The pursuits end soon enough, so the choice is between a run of miserable successes and some enjoyment. Severity is not the same as seriousness or sophistication.

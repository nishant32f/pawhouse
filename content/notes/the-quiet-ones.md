# Reviews reward visible work over essential invisible work

One engineer stayed late every night for a quarter, never complained and never missed a deadline. Khurana noticed only because the commits arrived at 3 AM. He mentioned a breakup and his father's surgery. Work was where he put what he could not say. Two months later he left for a company that noticed him first.

Every company has people who work across levels, doing L3 and L7 work at once, such as fixing the deploy pipeline while mentoring juniors or answering customer emails while rebuilding core systems. They cannot be ranked because they do what nobody thought to measure.

Reviews rate them low. They lack "scope" because they fix problems before they become projects, "leadership" because they lead by doing, and "impact" because preventing problems produces no OKRs. Matrices built to look fair reward visible work over invisible work, even when the invisible work is better.

At every startup Khurana joined, the most critical person was unpromotable because their work crossed every boundary. One designer rewrote every error message over three weekends and support tickets dropped 40%. She never mentioned it. After she left, two people were hired to do what she did unseen.

Strong performers expect to be seen without announcing it. Forced to advocate for themselves, they conclude the system rewards looking productive, and by the time someone asks for recognition they are already gone. Other quiet builders watching learn that being needed counts for nothing unless you are visible.

Recognition means seeing the person as well as the commits. That means equity before they ask, titles that do not fit the matrix, or asking about their father instead of the feature. These people leave when they realize the system measures only what is visible. When someone's commits arrive at 3 AM, check on the person before the code.

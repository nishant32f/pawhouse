# Bad memories fade faster than good ones

The feeling attached to a memory, good or bad, weakens over time, but the bad fades faster. Some bad memories turn good. Good memories rarely turn bad.

The reason is that a bad event switches on a psychological immune system with four steps:

- Rationalise. "Why would I want to be with someone who doesn't want to be with me?"
- Downplay. "Breakups happen all the time in high school, it's no big deal."
- Distance. "I never liked her that much anyway!"
- Distract. "I'm gonna go play video games."

These work like emotional antibodies and reduce the pain. Nobody applies them to good memories, so those keep their feeling.

Adam Mastroianni calls "Tragedy + time = comedy" the closest thing psychology has to a chemical equation.

So when things go badly, you will probably laugh about it later. Fearing how you will feel about it in the future adds to the present discomfort.

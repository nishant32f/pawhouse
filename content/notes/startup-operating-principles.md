# Startup operating principles

A startup succeeds by combining strong people, real customer value, disciplined product ownership and scaling systems that keep learning while increasing leverage. Most complexity is a variation on people quality, customer outcomes, product accountability or financial discipline.

## Three imperatives

The first is good people who are trained, trusted and equipped. The second is customers who are helped and willing to buy. The third is enough money to sustain and compound the work. Financial performance usually follows customer value, which usually follows people quality.

## People

- Hire for attitude before experience and education. Curiosity, ownership, cultural fit and learning velocity decide who thrives in ambiguity. See [[hiring|Hire for traits and treat a split decision as a no]].
- Hold real one-on-ones instead of disguised status reviews. The person should be able to tell whether they were heard, supported and understood.
- Leadership is creating conditions where capable people make good decisions repeatedly.

## Money

Negotiate like it is your own money and pay like it is theirs. If vendors have to chase payment updates, the system is failing.

## Product

You are not the customer. Ground product work in real users, workflows and willingness to pay.

A strong product manager acts like the CEO of the product. They know the market, product, competition and customer. They own product success and define the what and leave out every detail of the how. They write clearly, focus the team on customers and revenue, anticipate serious flaws before they become emergencies, and decompose problems. Bad product management hides behind excuses, informal direction, constant fire-fighting and feature comparison.

Prioritise accuracy over speed, and speed over perfection. Ship accurate work fast enough to learn.

## Scaling

The founder does everything at first. Learn from customized work until reusable components emerge.

1. Customize with high customer contact.
2. Build tooling that increases field leverage.
3. Generalize one level above individual requests.
4. Land with one compelling outcome.
5. Expand into adjacent value.
6. Abstract the repeated pattern back into the platform.

Sell outcomes instead of installs. A demo should force a believable end-to-end workflow that creates desire as well as agreement. Measure the value delivered to the customer and the leverage gained by the product or field team.

If the first product or feature is not on the executive priority list, internal process can stall it. Executive support can be a technical dependency for adoption.

# Errors of omission cost more than they feel

Errors of commission (things you do) teach faster than errors of omission (things you do not do). Commission leaves evidence. Omission disappears into the background, so you feel the pain of trying and failing and rarely the cost of never finding out.

## Examples

- Betrayal stings. Refusing to trust leaves no scar from the love, partnership or help missed.
- A failed career move is obvious. Staying put and losing years is not.
- Leaving risks loneliness. Staying with someone who never makes you feel fully alive is an equal, unseen danger.
- A stupid remark stings. Never raising your hand has a cost nobody tallies.
- One bad investment feels like a catastrophe. Never investing compounds unseen.
- Rejection is remembered. Decades of regret over not asking go unnamed.
- Bad writing embarrasses. Never writing costs more.
- A failed company is feared. Someone else launching your idea a decade later is not.
- Yes to the wrong thing gets attention. Erosion by habitual no does not.

## History

- Kodak invented digital photography in the 1970s. Executives shelved an engineer's cassette-tape prototype for thirty years for fear of hurting film sales, and Kodak went bankrupt in 2012.
- Darwin held natural selection from 1838 for twenty years, until Alfred Russel Wallace independently reached it and pushed him to act.
- After Sarajevo, Austria could have paused, Germany could have told Austria to stand down and Britain could have stated its red lines. Everyone waited for someone else, and a local killing became a world war.

## Making the cost visible

Commission teaches in days, omission in decades. Williamson says "People make commission errors if they don't think enough. People make omission errors if they think too much." Overthinkers should ask what not acting has cost, is costing and will cost. Otherwise "Our minds would rather endure a familiar misery than gamble on an unfamiliar freedom." Commission costs pride in the short term. Omission costs years.

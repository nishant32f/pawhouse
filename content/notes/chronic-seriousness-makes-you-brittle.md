# Chronic seriousness makes you brittle

Seriousness gives a large advantage in bursts, but chronic seriousness makes you rigid and prone to blow-up. Playfulness protects long-term persistence. The goal is dynamic persistence.

Joe Hudson asks what this would be like if it were 10% more enjoyable. It fits a meeting, a talk with a partner, sport, or something you hate.

> "Make a sense of humour your default." ~ Matthew McConaughey

## What winning should feel like

Check whether winning feels like happiness and satisfaction or only relief. After a while, how you win and how you feel during it matter more than whether you win. How readers are affected matters more than how many there are. Outcomes matter more than inputs, but the felt experience is what you remember. You do things for the experience of having done them.

Optimising for how you feel detaches you from the outcome and also drives outcomes best. If it does not, you still enjoy it. If worry is your proof of seriousness, letting go feels irresponsible instead of free.

> "Who could you be in the absence of your concerns?" ~ Peter Crone

## Depth and conscience

Emotional pain teaches and will not kill you. Feeling less might be easier, but depth of thought is why you get the outcomes you want. Treat goals and attachments as hypotheses to test. You have handled everything so far, perhaps owing more to being capable than to grasping, controlling, fearing and ruminating.

Ask whether the goal is to survive, thrive or flourish. Past a certain material comfort you do it for yourself, and your conscience knows whether you are honest. Be the person your mum thinks you are and the role model your younger self wanted. Ted Hughes, via Visakan Veerasamy, measured respect by feeling:

> "by the degree of feeling they can register, the voltage of life they can carry and tolerate ~ and enjoy."

# Nobody is thinking about you as much as you think

## The looking-glass self

In 1902 Charles Horton Cooley described the looking-glass self. Your sense of who you are largely reflects what you imagine others think of you, in three steps.

1. You imagine how you appear to others rather than how you actually appear.
2. You imagine their judgment of it.
3. You feel pride, shame, confidence or anxiety about that imagined judgment, and it shapes who you become. Think people see you as funny and you lean into it. Think they see you as boring and you may withdraw.

The mirror is inaccurate because everyone else is occupied with their own reflection. The embarrassing moment you still cringe at from years ago is one nobody else remembers. Likes and view counts are an algorithmic, curated mirror that shows what keeps people scrolling.

You cannot stop caring what others think, since people are social. Cooley's point was to let you choose when to play the game. Ask whether a perceived judgment is what they think or your insecurity, and choose mirrors that reflect who you want to become.

Genuine enemies are rare. The world is mostly indifferent, so the harshest judge is usually your own voice, covered in [[your-inner-critic-was-an-outer-voice|Your inner critic was once an outer voice]].

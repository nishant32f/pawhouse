# People who can't feel pleasure hide in meaning and delayed gratification

Viktor Frankl's principle:

> "When a man can't find a deep sense of meaning, they distract themselves with pleasure" ~ Viktor Frankl

Lack of meaning sends people to temporary relief in superficial pursuits. A second group has the opposite problem. When a person cannot find a deep sense of pleasure, they distract themselves with meaning. Chris Williamson raises this inverse with Arthur Brooks.

If ease, grace, joy and playfulness do not come easily, one solution is to ignore moment-to-moment happiness and always pursue hard things. You become a world champion at the marshmallow test and call permanent delayed gratification noble because you struggle to feel gratified.

Bill Perkins says extreme delayed gratification results in no gratification. It looks like transcending the need for pleasure. In practice it avoids the fact that you struggle to feel joy and promises happiness tomorrow, which never arrives, like running toward the horizon.

> "The price of anything is the amount of life you exchange for it" ~ Thoreau

Many people save up enjoyment that they never collect. In anticipation it sounds like building toward an impressive moment that justifies the pain. In retrospect it may feel like a series of miserable successes with no time spent enjoying the years. Brooks adds that strivers are often addicted to the satisfaction of achievement and keep chasing the next win because they cannot feel enjoyment.

Oliver Burkeman's remedy is to do some of what you care about now, since "Life's duties will never be out of the way."

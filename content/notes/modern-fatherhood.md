# Present fathers are more exhausted and more fulfilled

Derek Thompson, quoted in Chris Williamson's Three Minute Monday newsletter, writes that "Compared to their Boomer parents, childcare time among Millennial dads has more than doubled." Compared with their Silent Generation grandparents it has nearly quadrupled. Thompson says few parts of daily life have changed more in the last half-century than how parents, and fathers especially, spend their time. His summary is that the new American dad is more present, more exhausted and more satisfied with life.

The role has moved from distant provider to active daily participant, with more childcare, more emotional presence and more domestic load. Presence tires you and also gives meaning, so a life can be heavier and better at once.

- Presence is costly, and treating it as effortless hides the work of modern parenting.
- Comparing generations should account for changed expectations as well as changed character.
- Satisfaction comes from meaningful load, and zero load does not produce it.

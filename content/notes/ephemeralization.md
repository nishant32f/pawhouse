# Doing more with less frees capacity to attempt more

Buckminster Fuller's "ephemeralization" is the pattern of doing more and more with less and less, until eventually you can do everything with nothing. It is progress through dematerialization, with stronger, lighter, cheaper and more capable systems replacing heavier ones.

Technology expands human capability by cutting the material, labor, time and coordination cost of action. Fuller's examples include stone bridges, iron trusses and steel cables. Each generation spans more with less.

Garry Tan applies it to intelligence work. AI lets more get done with fewer people, less time, less coordination and lower marginal cost. That can mean a higher ambition ceiling instead of a smaller organization.

When a tool lets you do more with less, use the freed capacity to attempt the "more" as well as cutting the "less".

# You don't need self-belief before acting

Chris Williamson asks whether belief or action comes first. "Fake it until you make it" is hard for introspective people with low self-belief and high standards. His alternative is to make it until you believe it. Ryan Holiday's version is to generate evidence.

You can think you are unworthy of something and still get it, expect your effort to go badly and still succeed, doubt your talent and still end up somewhere your 20-year-old self could not imagine. The world sees only your actions and never the internal state behind them. Williamson wonders whether virtue counts if you felt you had no other choice, and leaves the question unresolved. He says you can reach a surprising place however motivated you feel.

## Belief as a byproduct

Confidence results from action. Ask what evidence a confident person would have and build it: one finished workout, published essay, honest conversation, shipped product, kept promise, or difficult thing completed despite doubt. The smallest completed action persuades more than another internal debate. It is the mechanism in [[self-trust-from-kept-promises|Self-trust is built by keeping small promises]].

## Waiting as avoidance

If belief is the requirement, introspective people wait for clarity, confidence, healing, motivation or certainty. That waiting is refined avoidance, the pattern in [[preparation-becomes-avoidance|Preparation becomes avoidance once it delays execution]]. Emotional pain here shows depth and does not mean a wrong path. Act with curiosity.

## Where this disagrees with another note

[[move-like-it-will-work-out|Moving as if it will work out keeps you moving]] says belief comes first and doubt leads to self-sabotage. Williamson's view here is that belief is optional and evidence can come first. Both say keep going.

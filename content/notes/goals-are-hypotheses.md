# Treat goals as hypotheses to test

Chris Williamson names a trap common among overachievers.

> "For many of us, goals are contracts to self-criticise until we succeed."

The goal stops being a direction and becomes a threat. The person must succeed permanently or feel miserable. Ambition then stops expressing agency and becomes a source of constant self-pressure.

In "An ode to people who don't believe in themselves," he offers a different stance. Your goals, your desire for attainment and your attachments are hypotheses to be tested. They are not ideologies to be proved. A hypothesis can fail without making you unacceptable.

Emotional pain is information and depth. It is no proof that you are failing. You can doubt the process, question your talent, feel you never work hard enough, and still end up somewhere your 20-year-old self could not imagine. Winning is meant to feel like joy as well as the end of fear (see [[chronic-seriousness-makes-you-brittle|Chronic seriousness makes you brittle]]).

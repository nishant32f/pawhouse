# Avoiding boredom with screens makes life feel emptier

Arthur Brooks describes a loop. Boredom is uncomfortable, so you distract yourself, which lowers boredom tolerance, so life feels less meaningful and you scroll more. Alcohol follows the same path, since anxiety and boredom predict use and use worsens both. Phone addiction is harder to spot because it is socially normal, and workaholism is worse because it is applauded.

A simulated life removes local boredom and is empty overall. A meaningful life contains boredom. Brooks's great-grandfather behind a mule was probably bored minute to minute, inside a real life of obligations, family and land.

## Breaking a loop

1. Get angry. Recognise that the habit controls you and feel the anger of being tired of it.
2. Use an algorithm, a concrete behavioural protocol for stopping.
3. Learn to live with yourself again. Addiction often means you did not like being home in your own head.

Brooks quit drinking at 38 after seeing alcohol as a vacation from himself. The hard part was being awake and alone with himself.

For phones he recommends boundaries over abstinence.
- No phone in the first hour of the day (except real emergencies) or the last hour before sleep, none in the bedroom ever, and none at meals or in classrooms.
- Phone-free zones and times, and annual fasts including a 96-hour technology fast. His four-day retreats begin with mental screaming, then calm, then relief.

Learning to be bored means sitting on a train, looking at a tree and being alive. Repetitive prayer, meditation, mindfulness and mind-wandering activate the default mode network, and mind-wandering reliably leads to meaning.

## The mirror

Social media is a virtual mirror of likes, mentions and self-image, and Zoom has people staring at themselves. Brooks says this induces narcissism and blocks meaning. A former fitness influencer deleted his accounts, enrolled in acupuncture school, removed every mirror and showered in the dark for a year to stop obsessing over his abs. Only then did he feel free.

# The mind holds many competing selves

Sam Harris says "Living an examined life is just one insult after another, you have to maintain a sense of humour." The mind has no shame and will think anything. The person in the mirror "is a congress of selves, and many of them are total assholes." You did not create yourself, your culture or your consciousness, so the question is what to do next with the mind you have. It is a moral, psychological and existential navigation problem, and there are tools for becoming a wiser and happier pilot.

Chris Williamson calls it an internal multiple personality disorder everyone has. More than half the voices act like villains, and the best versions of us are overridden by judgemental, harsh, emotional and juvenile ones that push us against our interests. Experience follows the story you tell about it, so a mostly unkind narrator makes happiness hard. In Harris's words, "Wisdom is a matter of making your mind your friend."

## Hidden inner life

Five lines on hidden pain come from a carousel by @mindset.unchained, with the attributions the post gives.

- The Joker, "Someday someone will break you so badly that you'll become unbreakable."
- Unknown (paraphrase), smiles and kindness can hide secrets, tears and deep pain.
- Thomas Shelby (paraphrase), pursue what makes you happy because people will talk regardless.
- Thomas Shelby, "Behind my smile is everything you'll never understand."
- The Joker (paraphrase), we stop fearing monsters under our beds when we recognise them within ourselves.

They are not psychological rules. Pain does not make anyone invulnerable, and recognising difficult feelings does not mean being defined by them. The useful part is self-awareness and compassion without romanticising damage, since appearances rarely show an inner life and public opinion should not decide your choices.

# Problem-solving capacity must keep up with problem creation

> "The only constraint on our imagination is management capacity." Travis Kalanick

Ambition often fails because the organization creates problems faster than it can solve them. Kalanick, speaking with David Senra, calls this the meta problem.

```text
problem-solving capacity ≥ problem-creation rate
```

Launching a city, entering China, building a robotics stack, adding a business unit or entering a regulated market all create future problems, some months away. A founder should predict their shape, create the right ones and build capacity to absorb them. If the equation holds, widen scope. If it breaks, slow problem creation and add capacity (leaders, judgment, process, recruiting, tooling, decision clarity). Inspiration does not fix it.

## Problems as material

Kalanick treats problems as a mathematician treats questions. A founder without hard problems is probably not working on something important enough. Interesting problems create leverage when solved, and chaos creates motion without learning. The skill is choosing difficulty deliberately. Treating uncontrolled problems as ambition is the failure. "Super pumped" is his term for infectious enthusiasm about a hard, strange problem people call impossible, and it needs capacity behind it.

## Fewest rules

Leadership is finding the line between order and chaos. Too much of either gives a slow, demoralized team. His rule is the fewest rules that stay out of chaos. At Uber, no city launched until Kalanick approved pricing. Pricing reflects regulation, car type, driver economics, demand, geography and strategy, so one rule taught local teams the whole business without a large process. His role is "problem solver in chief". He works on the most impactful problems nobody else is solving, and each leader does the same for their area.

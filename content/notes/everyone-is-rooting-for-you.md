# Most people are rooting for you

Most people are not hoping you fail. They are hoping their trust in you was a good decision.

Denzel Rust, quoted by Chris Williamson on Modern Wisdom, lists the cases. Parents want a great son. A wife wants a great husband. A boss wants the hire to be a slam dunk. A first date wants it to go well. An audience hopes a joke will have a hilarious punch line. Rust's conclusion is that proximity to anyone reflects on them, so the deck is never stacked against you and your failures are completely your own.

This does not mean life is fair or everyone is kind. Many ordinary interactions start with goodwill because the other person's taste, judgment, generosity or hope is tied to your success.

The hard part is accountability. If the room hoped you would win, "everyone was against me" is harder to maintain.

## Using it

- Treat social pressure as latent support until shown otherwise.
- Enter rooms assuming people want competence, honesty, humor and follow-through.
- If the deck was not stacked against you, improve the hand you played, without cruelty toward yourself.
- Ask where you are mistaking neutral hope for hostile judgment.

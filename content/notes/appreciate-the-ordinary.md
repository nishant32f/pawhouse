# Appreciation of ordinary things is a practice

Ordinary life wisdom is noticing and valuing simple things that are easy to overlook, such as warmth, smiles, care, effort, progress and the fact of being here at all.

The list can be long, and naming it is part of the practice.
- The sun warming you when you walk outside.
- Someone's smile, and people who tell you they care.
- The effort other people give you.
- How it feels to accomplish what you thought you couldn't.
- A mind racing with ideas at 3:00 a.m., which means you are still dreaming.
- Catching yourself smiling for no reason.
- Who you are, being here for a reason, and being wanted and loved.

Blessings count however small, and so do the lessons you have endured. You are constantly evolving, and the position you are in is not your final destination.

William Martin's poem [[children-and-striving|Children and Striving]] applies this to raising children. Do not ask them to strive for an extraordinary life. Help them find the wonder of an ordinary one by showing them tomatoes, apples and pears, grief for pets and people, and the touch of a hand. Martin ends with "The extraordinary will take care of itself."

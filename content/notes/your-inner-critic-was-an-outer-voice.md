# Your inner critic was once an outer voice

An inner voice is an outer voice that got internalised. Harsh self-criticism often began outside you and does not describe your true nature.

Goya's line "The sleep of reason produces monsters" frames it. Each of us has monsters. We should manage them with the respect owed to something that can kill us, building strong cages of solid, kind arguments. Night-time monsters get less vicious as life gets more reasonable and serene. With enough gentleness, we remember enough about ourselves to be unafraid. We know we are safe and worthy of tenderness, even when the monsters strain at their collars in the dead of night. The way out is to go through it, taking more of the thing that poisons you until it becomes a tonic.

## The enemy is behind you

A young MP touring Parliament with Winston Churchill called the MPs on the other benches "the enemy." Churchill reportedly said "That's the opposition, dear boy. The enemy is behind you."

Chris reads it as a point about how we presume the world is out to get us. That makes a good motivational story (David Goggins says as much) but is literally false. The world is indifferent and people are too caught up in their own lives to consider you. "We would care far less about what other people thought about us if we realised how rarely they do." Sometimes you become collateral damage, but real enemies barely exist for almost anyone.

The voice most able to hurt you is in your head. It knows your weaknesses, fears, shame and guilt, and scolds you for falling short even when you did your best. Seeing this gives you more control, though you still have to deal with the world.

# AI is a reason to attempt bigger projects

"Don't boil the ocean" keeps teams focused and prevents scope creep in normal times. Garry Tan argues superintelligence ends normal times.

> Artificial Superintelligence means it's time to boil the ocean. We'll start with a few lakes first.

Tan says "Our fear of the future is directly proportional to how small our ambitions are." If the plan is the same work, AI looks like a threat. If the plan is something much bigger, AI is leverage.

## Old game and new game

The old game is 5% efficiency gains, 2% margin improvements and lower cost by firing people, optimizing the 1.05x present. The new game builds for the 10x future and asks what was impossible before. Tan's examples:

- Why can't an endowment aim for 50% net IRR instead of 10%?
- Why can't a startup deliver a service 100x better than the incumbent?
- Why can't we have fusion energy?
- Why can't we talk to every user and understand every product bug?

He says these are engineering problems with paths to solutions. Another is what it would take to build a product people would happily pay 10x for.

## Who acts

Workers should become builders and start a business. Management and capital should be "10x more hardcore" on aspiration. Tan expects more jobs. The desire for more things is limitless, and technology meets more of it when people can prompt it themselves. He grounds this in [[ephemeralization|Fuller's ephemeralization]] and [[jevons-paradox|Jevons Paradox]]. Jevons needs agency. Capital and management must choose to boil lakes and oceans instead of letting committees shrink the ambition. Startups suit this because they move fast under radical uncertainty and build for the 10x future while others optimize for the 1.05x present. Use AI to ask whether the plan is too small.

# Behaviour has a proximate mechanism and an ultimate reason

> "Natural selection favours certain behaviours but doesn't necessarily favour us having explicit awareness of why we do what we do" ~ Dr Tania Reynolds

Ultimate explanations say why a behaviour exists. Proximate explanations say how it works. The proximate reason is the immediate "reason to" act. The ultimate reason is the "reason why" evolution shaped the behaviour that way. Understanding behaviour needs both.

| Behaviour | Proximate (reason to) | Ultimate (reason why) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sex | Feels good, pleasure, love | Creates offspring and continues the genetic line |
| Eating fats and sugars | Tastes good | Ancestrally rare, energy-rich, high-calorie food improved survival chances |
| Preferring symmetrical faces | Looks attractive | Symmetry is genetically harder to produce and signals better genes for offspring |

The two reasons do not contradict each other.

Seeing the ultimate explanations can make us feel less agentic and less in control of our desires, and more cynical, contrived and manipulative. Williamson's answer is humour. You are a sovereign creature who feels you have your own will. You are also largely driven by preferences millennia old, which is absurd enough to laugh at.

> "Ultimately happiness comes down to choosing between the discomfort of becoming aware of your mental afflictions, or the discomfort of becoming ruled by them"

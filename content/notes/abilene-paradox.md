# A group can choose what nobody wants

The Abilene paradox is a group collectively choosing something nobody individually wants. It is a failure to manage agreement. Disagreement is not the problem. People read silence as enthusiasm, and they go along because they fear being the difficult one.

The group does not lack objections. It lacks a safe way to surface them. Each person thinks "I do not want this, but everyone else seems to," so the group builds a consensus around a decision with no real owner. The false belief is "everyone else wants this". [[we-misjudge-what-others-believe|We misjudge what others believe]] covers the two related errors.

People are often more afraid of social friction than of a bad outcome. The decision becomes about preserving harmony, avoiding embarrassment or not being seen as the blocker. The group gives up truth for politeness and pays for it in wasted effort.

## Warning signs

- Lots of nodding and little energy.
- Nobody owns the decision.
- Dissent appears only after the decision fails.
- Everyone assumed someone else wanted it.
- People call it "what the group wanted" but cannot name who wanted it.

## Correction

Asking "Does everyone agree?" is not enough. Make private objections easy to reveal before the group commits. Useful questions:

- Who privately disagrees?
- What would make this a bad idea?
- If we were choosing alone, would we still choose this?
- Is anyone supporting this only because they think others want it?

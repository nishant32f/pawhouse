# Self-awareness can simulate futures faster than action resolves them

Chris Williamson reads Hamlet's "Thus conscience does make cowards of us all" as a point about consciousness rather than morality. Imagination lets people emotionally pre-live embarrassment, rejection, loss and moral failure, so they hesitate.

Self-awareness has benefits and costs. It prevents stupid action and makes people reflective and ethical. It can also produce simulated futures faster than action can settle them, and the result is [[omission-errors-cost-more|errors of omission]] such as the unstarted business, the unasked question, the unsent message and the life never tested.

For overthinkers, courage is not clean certainty. In Williamson's words, "Courage isn't about thinking clearly. It's about moving while things are still unclear." And "A life can be deeply examined and still never lived."

Williamson's remedy is movement. More reflection does not help.

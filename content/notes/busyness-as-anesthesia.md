# Busyness and achievement can numb what you would otherwise feel

Busyness numbs emotion. It keeps you moving too fast to feel loneliness, poor decisions, relational damage or misalignment. When the busyness stops, the feelings remain. That can feel like regression, but it is often the first honest contact with what the busyness was hiding.

Arthur Brooks says ambitious people are exposed to this because achievement is a respectable way to avoid being alone with yourself. Strivers use work, applause, alcohol, screens, success or pornography as anesthesia. The internal monologue is "don't leave me alone in here." Workaholism is more dangerous than most habits because the culture applauds it. A life built on numbing and overwork can reach its goals while losing the capacity to feel them.

Achievement also gives clean numbers. It is easy to count emails sent or hours on calls, and hard to work out what you want and whether you are moving toward it. The habit of being busy with tasks you would rather skip becomes linked with success, and the "got stuff done" feeling means you never ask whether you are using your potential. See [[operator-to-strategist|Moving from operator to strategist means giving up busyness as identity]].

Achievement cannot make up for the absence of safe relationships. A successful career with a miserable home life can still add up to a miserable life, so choose carefully what you are sacrificing for.

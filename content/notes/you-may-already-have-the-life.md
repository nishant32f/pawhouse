# You may already have the life you are working to earn

An American investment banker with a Harvard MBA meets a Mexican fisherman with several large yellowfin tuna, caught quickly. He stopped because he has enough for his family, and spends the rest of his time sleeping late, fishing a little, playing with his children, taking siestas with his wife Maria, and sipping wine and playing guitar with village friends each evening.

The banker proposes fishing more, buying a bigger boat, then a fleet, then skipping the middle-man with a cannery, moving to Mexico City, Los Angeles and New York to run it. In 15 to 20 years, an IPO and millions. "Then what?" You could retire to a small coastal fishing village, sleep late, fish a little, play with your kids, take siestas with your wife and play guitar with friends in the evenings.

The plan ends in the life the fisherman already has, so the 15 to 20 years are the price of getting back to the start.

## What money is for

The dream is not luxury. It is being able to say "Treat's on me" without thinking twice, with a warm home, a fridge stocked the way a five-year-old would stock it and a tray of chocolates for anyone to grab. It is helping a friend before they ask, sending a care package to someone too tired to reach out, and buying all the pencils from a kid on the street so they can go home early. The aim is to have enough to give and to comfort, and to make ordinary moments feel special.

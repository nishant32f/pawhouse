# People read understanding into simple pattern-matching

The Eliza effect is the tendency to read far more understanding into computer responses than exists. It is named after Joseph Weizenbaum's 1966 chatbot ELIZA, which imitated a Rogerian psychotherapist using simple pattern matching and reflection.

Weizenbaum was shocked when his secretary asked him to leave the room so she could have a "private conversation" with ELIZA. She knew it was a program reflecting her words back, and she still felt heard and understood.

## Why it happens

We are wired for social connection. Our brains evolved to detect agency and intention everywhere, because mistaking a rustling bush for a predator is better than missing a real one. The same pattern-seeking extends to machines. When a system responds in context, even superficially, we fill the gaps with assumed comprehension and project depth onto output that has none.

## Today

Current language models produce human-like text and trigger the effect more strongly than ELIZA did. People attribute consciousness, emotion and understanding to what remains pattern matching.

This is not necessarily a problem. Therapeutic chatbots can give real comfort and AI assistants can be useful. Awareness helps keep perspective on what the systems are versus what they feel like.

Knowing about the effect does not make us immune. Even Weizenbaum occasionally forgot ELIZA's nature during conversations.

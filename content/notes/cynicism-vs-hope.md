# Cynicism protects against disappointment and excuses inaction

> "On social media, fools feel clever by mocking bigger fools, and scoundrels feel virtuous by condemning bigger scoundrels. We feel good simply by portraying others as worse. In an age of anxiety & inaction, many seek self-esteem not in their own successes, but in other's failures" ~ Gurwinder Bhogal

Williamson is bored of cynicism on the internet. Comment sections fill with self-defeating, world-hating nihilists who insist the world is against them and that people with goals are the problem. Their faux-virtue rests on others' shortcomings, and pointing at everyone else's public failures keeps their own actions from scrutiny.

Cynicism is a guarded response. It guards against disappointment by striking first at a perceived threat. If you tell yourself all women are bad, you seek no relationship and never feel rejection. If you tell yourself everything is bad and will never improve, you are excused from trying anything. Fatalism gets called pragmatism, hope gets framed as pathetic and optimism as delusion. He calls it sour grapes at an existential level. If everything is awful and you know it for a fact, the people acting as if things can be better are the dumb ones. The upside of never trying is never feeling the pain of failure.

He does not want that influence in his life.

> "It's good to criticise the vile aspects of the world, but there is nothing more vile than to do nothing about it and convince others to do the same." ~ Ryan Lazarus

Believing in hope is a better way to live.

> "Choosing to see the good in things (while not being naïve) is a superpower" ~ Chasing Adaptation

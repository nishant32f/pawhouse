# Optimising rest turns leisure into work

Insecure overachievers have trouble telling the few areas that need full vigilance from the many that do not. They bring maximum intensity to ordinary tasks, relaxation, hobbies and self-improvement. How much you should care about something is not "the maximum, all the time, for everything."

Chris Williamson's example is passing out during breathwork because if two-minute breath holds were good, three-minute holds must be better.

> "Win what dude."

Relaxation, meditation and hobbies become arenas to perfect, and the thing meant to restore you becomes another thing you are judged on.

> "In the process of trying to be perfect you're thoroughly ruining the enjoyment of whatever you're supposed to be enjoying by turning leisure into labour." ~ Chris Williamson

There may be no problem to fix. Oliver Burkeman's alternative:

> "Pour your finite time and energy into something infinitely more absorbing than trying to keep life under control, which is actually living it."

## Atelic leisure

Arthur Brooks says strivers need real leisure. It is activity that creates value without external compensation, as Josef Pieper describes in *Leisure: The Basis of Culture*. It means deepening relationships, deepening your spiritual or philosophical life, and learning things you do not need to learn.

Chris tells of a friend told to take up watercolour painting without trying to improve. He started optimising brushes, classes and progression at once, and the coach had to stop him. The point was an atelic activity, done for its own sake.

Brooks ties this to Aristotle. Real friendship is atelic. Useful friends are transactional, and real friends are valued without regard to use. If you turn everything into improvement, monetisation or status, you strip out the love.

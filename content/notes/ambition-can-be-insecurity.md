# Outsized ambition is often powered by an outsized wound

Alain de Botton says all success is based on unhappiness, since a person fully at ease would not get out of bed. Many ambitions are insecurities and lives are often driven by shame, as [[ambition-vs-entitlement|Ambition expects you to close the gap and entitlement expects others to]] also notes.

## Where the drive comes from

- Thinking starts with frustration. The human animal is lazy and a problem moves it. Psychotherapists say thought begins when the breast does not come. Thought tends to match the size of the obstacle.
- Outsized achievement is powered by an outsized problem and shows what it is. Someone who makes a lot of money labors under deprivation. De Botton wrote *Essays in Love* at 22 because relationships were a problem for him.
- Elon Musk and Trump are powered by unhappiness, in Trump's case restlessness. Early losses, such as a parent dying or going bankrupt, tend to supply drive.

## Fame

- An exaggerated wish for fame often compensates for feeling invisible or injured. It is a wish for strangers to admire you, and it is about a lack of love.
- Society feeds it. The first question people get is "What do you do for a living?" We work partly for an answer that gets us seen and respected, and distinction becomes a competition.
- Fame brings envy. The most attack-sensitive people become famous, and the envious audience enjoys attacking them. A scandal brought De Botton down early in his career.
- A child who felt at the center of the world, even at one year old, can tolerate not being at the center. He sees more danger in under-spoiling than spoiling. The School of Life says a well-parented child has no interest in being famous.
- Content, at-ease people are never heard of. They felt seen without being famous.

# Compassion that avoids truth does harm

Toxic compassion puts short-term emotional comfort ahead of truth, reality and long-term flourishing. It optimises for appearing good rather than doing good. Kindness that refuses reality can become harmful.

There is a recurring trade-off between saying what sounds compassionate now and doing what improves outcomes later. Avoiding someone's distress can feel empathetic in the moment and produce worse results over time.

## Performative empathy

Performative empathy is saying whatever makes you look caring, whether or not it helps. The internet widens the gap between words and actions, because public signals of virtue can earn more reward than private service. That makes it easier to attach to the perception of goodness instead of the reality.

## Test

- Is this true?
- Does this help over the long term?
- Am I avoiding discomfort or preventing harm?
- Would I still do this if nobody could see me?
- Am I protecting the person or my image of myself as compassionate?

## Limit

This is not an argument for cruelty. Truth without care becomes harshness, and care without truth becomes enabling. The target is honest compassion, which faces reality, looks at outcomes and tolerates short-term discomfort for long-term good.

People who prioritise saying good things may not be doing good things.

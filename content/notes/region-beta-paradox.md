# Tolerable situations keep you stuck longer than bad ones

If something is just okay, you tolerate it. If it were worse, you would act. This is the Region Beta paradox.

Williamson gives these examples:

- A job that is tolerable keeps you stuck.
- A relationship that is fine drags on.
- An apartment with some mould is bearable, and just bad enough to stay in.

You would be better off if things were worse, because then you would be forced to change. The long version of this is staying in years of misery to avoid a few minutes of pain, such as leaving an unhappy friendship, leaving an unfulfilling career, or having a difficult conversation.

If you keep making the same mistake, you are the problem. Getting older without getting better means relearning the same lesson. Sometimes you need to create your own discomfort to force the change.

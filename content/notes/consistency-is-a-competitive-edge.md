# Consistency is rare enough to be an edge

David Goggins says "It's so easy to be great nowadays, because most of the world is weak." Dana White says anyone even remotely a savage will run these people over and it is all there for the taking.

Chris Williamson grants that victim mentality is a real problem, since nobody wants their town or country to decline because people forgot how to take discomfort. His further point is that widespread fragility is a competitive advantage. The bar is low enough that some consistency, intentionality, resilience and a gram of talent put you ahead.

## Which traits

- Consistency is the most reliable trait among successful people. It does not guarantee success, but inconsistency guarantees failure.
- Choosing hard things regularly is probably second.
- Curiosity about what is on the other side of a question is a pursuit of truth.
- There has never been more opportunity and less competition.

## Limits on carrying others

Lifting others as you rise is tempting, and standing on the world's fragility can feel exploitative. Williamson sets a limit. Your efforts multiply by 10 with the right people and by 0 with the wrong ones. Someone who refuses to change habits, update their worldview or improve their life after you gave them tools and encouragement can stay where they are. It is not your job to exhaust yourself carrying people who do not want to change. He admits a sidekick can be hard to find.

Sebastian Junger says "Humans don't mind hardship, in fact they thrive on it; what they mind is not feeling necessary. Modern society has perfected the art of making people not feel necessary."

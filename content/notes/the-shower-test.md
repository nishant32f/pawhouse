# Where your mind goes in the shower shows what you care about

> "You can tell what you care about by what you think about in the shower."

Jimmy Carr says attention settles on what matters when external input disappears. With no feed, inbox, podcast or task list, in the shower or on a walk, the mind returns to the unresolved thing it cares about. That separates declared priorities from lived ones. Answers are often in the silence people avoid, so making room for solitude takes practice. What you think about all the time is a reliable indicator of what to pursue.

## Whose desire is it

The test filters borrowed wants, such as money because someone else has it, a relationship as a social prop or a career that photographs well. Carr asks whose desire it is. One that survives only in public may be a show put on for status. One that keeps returning in solitude is likelier yours. You choose which status game to play. The world rewards specialisation while schooling pushes breadth, which yields competence over excellence, so focus on what you are unusually good at.

## Choosing means excluding

An interesting life is exclusive, since choosing one life closes off others. Gifts differ, so decide what you want and accept the tradeoffs. Deciding comes first and earning it second, and most people never finish the first because their desires are borrowed.

## The package

> "If you want the life but not the lifestyle, you guarantee disappointment."

The outcome comes with the process. Carr does around eight shows a week on tour, tests new jokes constantly and treats the craft like gym training. Ideas come from mental wandering or from economics, politics and philosophy run through a joke lens. A musician's package may be 5 to 10 years of learning before performing, then 10 broke, uncertain tours. Enjoyment has to cover the unglamorous parts, and the pain is accepted without resentment. Inputs shape you too. You are "the average of the five podcasts you listen to the most", and algorithmic rabbit holes shift what seems normal.

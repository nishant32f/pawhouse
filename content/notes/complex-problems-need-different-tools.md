# Complex life questions can't be solved with tools for complicated problems

Brooks borrows Ian McGilchrist's two modes of attention.

- Left hemisphere. Analysis, mechanics, abstraction, measurement, execution and *how*.
- Right hemisphere. Relationship, mystery, beauty, wholeness, love, context and *why*.

Both are needed. The error is a category mistake. Brooks traces it to scientism, the belief that every meaningful problem is complicated and that enough analysis, money, apps or supplements will solve it.

Complicated problems are mechanical and solvable. Complex ones are living, relational and existential and must be lived with. A car is complicated. A marriage, a calling and a soul are complex. You can optimise a calendar, track a habit or A/B test a profile, and none of it says whether you are loved or your work matters. Brooks loves his marriage partly because it cannot be solved.

Science has solved real suffering such as infant mortality, infections and surgical hygiene, but that success should not be projected onto every domain. Purely material poverty policy such as UBI can strip away earned success by ignoring human motivation. Utopian attempts to reorder people against evolutionary reality tend to fail.

His alternative is *aporeia*, the Greek posture of sitting in puzzlement over unanswerable questions, as with Zen koans or late-night talks with friends. Let the question work on you.

## Love as the test case

Romantic love switches on the right hemisphere because it cannot be solved. Dating apps are a left-brain answer to a right-brain problem and do better with human friction such as friends choosing matches, group settings and real-world introductions. The chemistry (sex hormones, serotonin shifts, oxytocin, dopamine) can be described without being exhausted. Brooks says of his wife, "I just love her." In Diotima and Socrates' ladder of love, attraction to a beautiful other is the first rung toward the meaning of life. If you want meaning, he says, get your heart broken, since heartbreak forces big questions unless you numb it.

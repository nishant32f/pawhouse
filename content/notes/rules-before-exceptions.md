# Intuition is earned by following the rules first

> “The young man knows the rules, but the old man knows the exceptions.” — Oliver Wendell Holmes

Intuition is what discipline becomes after enough repetitions, training that has become invisible. Beginners need rules because they lack taste. Experts can make exceptions because they have internalised the pattern behind the rule. Breaking rules before learning them is usually just not playing the game, whatever the image of the savant innovator who bends the form.

Rules give structure while you learn. They supply the repetitions needed to notice when the standard pattern no longer fits, and they guard against your own lack of pattern recognition.

A Confucian trainee memorises shelves of archaic texts and learns bow angles and step lengths. Edward Slingerland on 3000-year-old Confucianism:

> “All of this rigour and restraint, however, is ultimately aimed at producing a cultivated, but nonetheless genuine, form of spontaneity. Indeed, the process of training is not considered complete until the individual has passed completely beyond the need for thought or effort.”

Principles scale better than rigid prescriptions once judgment can carry context, so one-size-fits-all advice worsens as someone develops. The danger is acting like an expert while still needing the constraints.

Some black-and-white rules work at any stage, such as sleeping with the phone outside the bedroom, not drinking alcohol for no reason and getting up at the same time every day. After a few years of momentum the work shifts from obedience to discernment.

"Just trust your gut" is suspicious advice when the gut is untrained.

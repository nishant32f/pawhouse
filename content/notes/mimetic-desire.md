# Most wants are borrowed from other people

Mimetic desire is wanting by imitation. The object can be money, a partner, a job, a body, a lifestyle or a social position. The mechanism is social. The thing becomes desirable because someone else wants it, has it or makes it legible as high status. That is why a desire can feel personal while being borrowed.

Borrowed wants produce expensive lives that do not fit. A person can chase the right-looking outcome, win the nearby status game and still feel untouched by the victory. The desire was real as social pressure but not necessarily as a life direction.

Many ambitions are copied from the status game nearby. Examples are money because someone else has it, a relationship as a social proof object and a career path because it photographs well. Jimmy Carr's question is "whose desire is this?" rather than "what do you want?" A desire that survives only in public may be a show put on for status, and one that keeps returning in solitude is more likely yours.

## Diagnostic

- Would I still want this if nobody could see it?
- Did I want this before I saw someone else praised for it?
- Am I attracted to the work or only to the status signal attached to the result?
- Does the desire survive solitude or only comparison?

# Advice amplifies what you already are

Guidance does not sculpt us into something new. It exaggerates who we already are. Those who least need a piece of advice take the most of it, and those who need it are immune. Chris Williamson calls the receptive ones Advice Hyperresponders.

## Examples

- "Don't be pushy with women" makes conscientious, anxious men more timid. Men who steamroll boundaries ignore it.
- "Just work harder" is absorbed by the insecure overachiever already working all day. The lazy person coasts past.
- "Men should open up more" is swallowed by sensitive, expressive men. The stoic who equates vulnerability with weakness ignores it.
- "Take more responsibility" makes people who think everything is their fault carry more. Blamers do not change.

Stories repeat it. Daedalus told the already reckless Icarus to keep to the middle path, and Icarus flew too high. The already romantic Don Quixote read knight tales as instruction and charged windmills. The already austere Buddha followed self-denial so literally he nearly starved, then created the Middle Way.

## Why

- People filter advice through existing traits, so it amplifies a predisposition.
- We take guidance that flatters our self-image as conscientious, virtuous or hardworking.
- The advice that lands hardest matches an inner fear. The anxious man hears that any move is too much, the overachiever that he is never enough, the self-blamer that he is guilty.

Good counsel misapplied can be worse than bad counsel or none. Self-improvement advice often goes to the people it harms most. Those who should hold back over-apply it, and those who could loosen up under-apply it.

"Work harder" is no universal cure. Low-discipline people may need structure, standards and effort. Insecure overachievers may need rest, softness and permission not to optimise everything. The skill is discernment, including when to stop listening.

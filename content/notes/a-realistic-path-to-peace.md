# A realistic goal is a few moments of peace a day

Almost nobody can move to a cave in the woods for a decade of silent retreat.

With enough meditation, mindfulness builds up. You get more ability to drop into the present moment, and small moments of calm insight sometimes appear. For Chris, this did not become lasting, self-sustaining enlightenment. He also does not find himself present without noticing.

What consistent practice does is train the ability to bring the mind back to the present. You learn to have moments during the day when the mind settles into the present. Then the moment ends, and you get it back later.

You will not stay in a permanent, blissful, non-dual state. You can string together some moments of peace so that a few times a day your mind rests where your feet are.

Chris used to count losing the state as failure, since it was not persistent enlightenment. He now thinks a better goal is a smaller one. Having mind and feet in the same place 5 or 10 times a day is a good start. Later it might be 15 or 20, which is both attainable and useful.

Aim for a few moments of peace today.

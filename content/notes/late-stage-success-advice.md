# Successful people give advice from the maintenance phase

Successful people tend to describe the strategies they use now, and leave out the ones that made them successful. Maintenance strategies are not creation strategies, so the advice misleads beginners. Ask what someone did at your stage. What they do now that they have won matters less.

Late-stage success often looks like balance, ease, detachment, leverage, early bedtimes, walking more, working less, transcending insecurity and not obsessing over outcomes.

Early-stage success often required obsession, long hours, sacrifice, insecurity, resentment, fear, brute-force learning and unglamorous work before leverage existed.

Both can be true. The mistake is treating the current strategy as the cause of the original outcome.

People forget the cost of the early phase. They remember the polished philosophy and forget the years of compulsion, anxiety, hunger and repetition that earned the freedom to be calmer later. Some advice is a luxury belief, easier to hold once you have done the hard part. "Balanced drive" is easier to recommend after years of imbalance produced reputation, money, leverage and options. It may suit someone who has arrived and mislead someone who has not started.

When evaluating advice, ask:

- What stage is this person in, and what stage am I in?
- Did this advice create their success or preserve it?
- What did they do at my level?
- What incentives do they have to tell a cleaned-up version of the story?

# Perseverance is keeping your behaviour intact after failure

Marc Andreessen, in *The Pmarca Guide to Career Planning, part 2: Skills and Education*, as captured from an a16z post:

> If you're going to be a high achiever, you're going to be in lots of situations where you're going to be quickly making decisions in the presence of incomplete or incorrect information, under intense time pressure, and often under intense political pressure. You're going to screw up — frequently — and the screwups will have serious consequences, and you'll feel incredibly stupid every time. It can't faze you — you have to be able to just get right back up and keep on going.

He calls this possibly the most valuable skill to learn, and says to start early.

Perseverance here means keeping your behaviour intact after failure has made you feel stupid. If the work matters, mistakes are part of the operating environment and not evidence that you should stop. The useful steps are to absorb the embarrassment, repair what can be repaired, learn what can be learned, and continue.

After a mistake under pressure, separate the event from the hit to your identity:

- What happened?
- What consequence needs immediate repair?
- What decision rule was wrong or missing?
- What would I do differently next time?
- What is the next useful action?

Shame should not become a second mistake.

## The no big deal mentality

A companion frame says nothing is a big deal. Rejection, failure and setbacks are part of the process and say nothing about your worth. Read each rejection as information, each failure as feedback, and each setback as redirection. Treating obstacles as personal attacks on your character adds suffering that serves no purpose.

# Hire for traits and treat a split decision as a no

A bad hire is worse than no hire, most of all at a startup. These notes come from about eight years of interviewing and hiring, plus readings. Core skill rounds are out of scope. Probe the traits in every round. Skills can be learned. Traits form the personality.

## Traits

**Smart.** Aptitude for technical problems. Recall of facts does not count. Good questions and flowing conversation are a good sign. Skills go obsolete in a couple of years, so look for someone who breaks an unfamiliar problem down, attacks each piece and reassembles it (first principles).

**Gets things done.** Smart people who cannot ship are a problem. Look for schedule-keeping and iteration over solutions that look good on paper. Let them reach a working solution, then see whether they iterate and keep assumptions from stripping the problem's scope.

**Aware.** Do they know the limits of their solution and of their knowledge? You cannot find an answer without knowing there is a problem. Self-awareness matters most. Glaring blind spots mean wrap up and politely send them home. Good candidates acknowledge a gap when shown evidence.

**Passionate.** A friend, Neelesh, says passion is transferable. Without it people rarely care or motivate others. Early hires must have it. A few 9 to 5 people are fine, too many make a slow corporate. It shows in voice, tone and excitement. Passionate people defend beliefs, sometimes wrongly, and self-awareness takes over when they see evidence.

**Articulate.** If they use big words, ask for an explanation fit for your grandmother. Someone who cannot put ideas across, from brilliance or introversion, is probably not a fit. Teams need effective communication.

**Leadership.** Avoid people who blame circumstances, bosses and colleagues and complain without fixing anything. Look for someone who overcomes inertia and takes charge, juniors included. Probe through reasons for leaving past jobs, experience and situational questions. It includes working through fuzziness and constraints (budgets, promotions, headcount, deadlines). Blaming too few resources for a missed deadline is easy. Working out what the team can achieve with what it has is hard.

**Invests in self.** Skills do not last, especially in technology. Check whether they follow blogs and know prominent people or companies in their field. Ask about meaningful hobbies (Netflix at home or a cafe 30+ km away does not count), favourite quotes and role models, as proxies for learning and passion.

## Interview length and timing

Interview for one hour. Less is insufficient unless it is a clear no, which can end politely at 40 minutes. More burdens both sides at scale. Decide about three minutes after the interview and record the decision and justification within 15 minutes, since information is lost after that.

## Decision

If you have trouble deciding, it is a no hire. Do not hire someone you are not entirely sure about. If the resume and phone screen work, about 20% of live interviews end in a hire. You will know when you find one. If you are not thrilled, move on.

## Questions to avoid

Do not ask illegal questions about race, religion, gender, nationality, origin, age, sexual orientation, veteran status, marital status, having or planning kids, or school.

# Is kindness you are forced into still kindness?

> "No one deserves to be praised for kindness if he does not have the strength to be bad." ~ La Rochefoucauld

The question is whether you are kind or just incapable of being bad. Williamson's tendency is to treat other people's emotional states as his responsibility. If you are not okay, then I am not okay, and your distress is a problem for me to solve.

After an amicable debate he rang a friend to check in. The friend said they were fine. He then started talking about wanting to dial back his people pleasing and his need to make sure everyone else is okay before himself. The friend stopped him: "be careful getting rid of that, the fact you rang to check on me is exactly one of the reasons I love you as a friend. Don't pathologise the kindest parts of your nature."

But he did not choose to check in. He had to. The impact was good, yet being compelled and less effortful seems to reduce the virtue.

That raises further questions.

- Is a good act more virtuous when it took more effort, when kindness was harder than meanness?
- If your nature compels a good act, is it more virtuous to deprogram the compulsion, become a worse friend for a while, and then bring the act back as a conscious choice?

Williamson calls that an unnecessarily effortful way to feel good about being kind, and has no answer.

# We misjudge what others believe

Groups often misread what people actually believe, want or support. Two errors cause most of the problem, and the [[abilene-paradox|Abilene paradox]] follows from them.

## False consensus

False consensus is overestimating how many others agree with you. Your own view becomes the reference point for everyone else. Typical phrases are "Everyone knows this," "Nobody actually likes that," "Any reasonable person would agree" and "This is just common sense."

The correction is to get actual data and diverse perspectives before assuming agreement.

## Pluralistic ignorance

Pluralistic ignorance is many people privately rejecting a norm while assuming everyone else accepts it. Each person performs agreement because they think they are alone in disagreeing, and that performance sustains the norm nobody wants.

Examples are students hiding confusion because nobody else asks questions, employees tolerating bad practices because everyone else seems fine, and people joining social behaviour they privately dislike.

The correction is to create safe ways to reveal private preferences.

## Comparison

| Concept | Error | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| False consensus | "Everyone agrees with me" | Overconfidence in support |
| Pluralistic ignorance | "I am the only one who disagrees" | Public compliance with private doubt |
| Abilene paradox | "Everyone else wants this" | Group action nobody wanted |

Never treat silence as evidence of agreement. Ask what people believe, want and are willing to sacrifice for.

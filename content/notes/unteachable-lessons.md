# The most important lessons sound like cliches until you live them

We refuse to learn some advice from instruction. We ignore warnings from elders, songs, literature and public scandals, thinking it may be true for them but not for me. They are always the big things, and most of life goes to learning firsthand what the previous generation warned about.

- Money will not make you happy.
- Fame will not fix your self-worth.
- The person you chase because they are attractive and hard to get is not someone you love.
- Nothing is as important as it feels while you are thinking about it.
- You will regret working too much.
- Worrying does not improve your performance.
- Your fears are mostly a waste of time.
- You should see your parents more.
- You will be fine after the breakup and grateful you ended it.
- It is fine to cut toxic people out of your life.

Everyone has heard this list and still falls for it, Williamson says, because each of us decides we are the exception. We believe our mental makeup, situation or history makes us immune, and we expect to get through without harm. Then we get hurt.

> "A cursed fact of the world is that the most important life lessons you learn are the hardest to communicate to others because they always sound like clichés." Nabeel Qureshi

Adam Mastroianni describes the result. "And you will then share a knowing look, the kind that can only occur between two people who have been hurt in exactly the same way." A voice in the back of your mind says "I told you so."

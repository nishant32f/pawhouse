# Wanting the outcome without its lifestyle guarantees disappointment

Max Barry: "It is a supremely cruel thing to have your mind conjure a desire which it is functionally unable to realise." Chris Williamson describes two versions.

**Talent without the resilience for the price.** A pop star has a strong voice and a debut album with strong buzz. She is pushed into the limelight with scrutiny, criticism and compliments. Her nervous disposition intensifies until anxiety stops her performing her own songs, each failure reinforces the fear, and the art that once freed her becomes a tormenter. Other examples are business owners who crack under pressure, sports stars who get the yips, and lawyers and traders who medicate to cope with the job.

**Desire without the work ethic.** A young man wants to leave the life he was born into. He consumes advice from effective thinkers and knows that applying himself would build a life he would be proud of. Every time he tries to discipline himself he fails. He cannot wake on time, focus on study or a plan, goes out partying when he should be locking in, and is pulled off track by side quests.

Both have the desire and the capacity but not the constitution to carry it through.

James Clear: "It doesn't make sense to continue wanting something if you're not willing to do what it takes to get it. If you don't want to live the lifestyle, then release yourself from the desire. To crave the result but not the process is to guarantee disappointment"

There are three options. Do what needs doing, drop the aspiration, or stay in neutral, tortured. Williamson calls the third worse than abandoning everything.

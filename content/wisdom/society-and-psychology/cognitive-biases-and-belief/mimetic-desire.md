# Mimetic Desire

Mimetic desire is wanting by imitation.

The object may be money, a partner, a job, a body, a lifestyle, or a social position. The hidden mechanism is social: the thing becomes desirable because someone else wants it, has it, or makes it legible as high status.

This is why desire can feel personal while being borrowed.

## Why It Matters

Borrowed wants create expensive lives that do not fit.

A person can chase the right-looking outcome, win the nearby status game, and still feel strangely untouched by the victory. The desire was real as a social pressure, but not necessarily real as a life direction.

## Diagnostic

Ask:

- Would I still want this if nobody could see it?
- Did I want this before I saw someone else praised for it?
- Am I attracted to the work, or only the status signal attached to the result?
- Does the desire survive solitude, or only comparison?

[[wisdom/personal-growth/self-worth-and-identity/the-shower-test|The Shower Test]] is one practical filter: when inputs disappear, copied desires often lose force while genuine preoccupations keep returning.

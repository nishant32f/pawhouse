# Abilene Paradox

The Abilene paradox is when a group collectively chooses something nobody individually wants.

It is a failure to manage agreement, not disagreement. Silence gets misread as enthusiasm. People go along because they fear being the difficult one.

## The Pattern

The group does not lack objections. It lacks a safe way to surface them.

Each person privately thinks, "I do not want this, but everyone else seems to." Because nobody wants to be difficult, the group manufactures consensus around a decision that has no real owner.

## Warning Signs

- lots of nodding, little energy
- nobody owns the decision
- dissent only appears after the decision fails
- everyone assumed someone else wanted it
- people describe the decision as "what the group wanted," but cannot name who actually wanted it

## Why It Happens

People are often more afraid of social friction than bad outcomes.

The decision becomes less about the thing itself and more about preserving harmony, avoiding embarrassment, or not being seen as the blocker. The group sacrifices truth for politeness, then pays for it in wasted effort.

## Correction

Do not ask only, "Does everyone agree?"

Ask:

- "Who privately disagrees?"
- "What would make this a bad idea?"
- "If we were choosing alone, would we still choose this?"
- "Is anyone supporting this only because they think others want it?"

Make private objections easy to reveal before the group commits.

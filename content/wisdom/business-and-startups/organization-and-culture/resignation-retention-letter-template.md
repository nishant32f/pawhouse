# Resignation Retention Letter Template

A useful letter pattern for when a strong teammate resigns and the honest answer is not passive acceptance, but a respectful fight for them.

## Use case

Use this when:

- a high-agency person resigns for an external opportunity
- you genuinely believe they are central to what is being built
- the relationship has enough trust for emotional honesty
- you want to communicate conviction without guilt-tripping them
- you want to show that retention is not merely compensation, but meaning, people, and shared future

## Why it works

The letter works because it does four things at once:

1. **It honors the decision.** It does not begin by attacking the resignation or dismissing the external opportunity.
2. **It makes the person feel seen.** “Great people… eager and unafraid of creating the future” names the employee’s identity, not just their output.
3. **It reframes retention as care.** The “fight” is not coercion; it is effort, commitment, and protection of what matters.
4. **It creates emotional contrast.** The external opportunity may be good, but the current mission is framed as something rare and unfinished.

## Adaptable template

Hi [Name],

I spent a few days thinking about your resignation and kept coming back to the idea that when something matters, you do not just let it pass quietly. You show up for it. You fight for it — not through pressure or conflict, but through effort, honesty, and a willingness to say what is true.

I genuinely love working with people who are eager, thoughtful, and unafraid of building the future. That is how I see you.

So although I am happy for you and respect the opportunity in front of you, I also have a hard time pretending I do not want to fight for you to stay. I believe we are at the cusp of something important, and I believe you are one of the people who can help shape it.

I do not want to guilt you or make this decision harder in an unfair way. But I would regret not telling you clearly: I value you, I believe in what we are building together, and I would love to explore whether there is a version of the future here that still feels exciting enough for you to choose.

If you are open to it, I would like to talk before you make this final.

## Tone notes

- Keep it personal, not corporate.
- Do not make the person responsible for your feelings.
- Do not imply betrayal.
- Do not over-index on money unless compensation is actually the issue.
- Make a real ask: one more conversation, one revised role, one clearer future.
- Only send this if you mean it. The note is too intimate for performative retention.

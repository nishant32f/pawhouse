# You Can't Simulate Meaning

Modern life can feel fake because much of it has become a functional simulation of the things that used to give life texture: connection, achievement, dating, friendship, status, purpose, beauty, even intimacy.

Arthur Brooks' argument on *Modern Wisdom* is blunt: the problem is not that people lack comfort, money, or success. The problem is that many people lack meaning.

Source: [Modern Wisdom #1109 - Arthur Brooks on Why Nothing Feels Real Anymore](/summaries/2026-06-15-modern-wisdom-arthur-brooks-nothing-feels-real)

## The Core Pattern

Algorithms can produce a life that is pleasant enough to prevent boredom and stimulating enough to keep attention, but not deep enough to nourish the person living it.

The substitutes look close enough to work in the short term:

- scrolling instead of attention
- online status instead of significance
- approval instead of love
- dating apps instead of embodied courtship
- gaming or metrics instead of contribution
- pornography or AI companionship instead of intimacy
- productivity dashboards instead of purpose
- constant input instead of contact with reality

The danger is not that these things are always evil. The danger is that they are often *near substitutes*: they scratch the surface of a need while preventing the deeper need from being met.

## Left-Brain Tools, Right-Brain Questions

Brooks borrows from Ian McGilchrist's distinction between modes of attention:

- the left-brain mode handles analysis, mechanics, abstraction, measurement, execution, and `how`
- the right-brain mode handles relationship, mystery, beauty, wholeness, love, context, and `why`

The modern error is trying to answer right-brain questions with left-brain tools.

You can optimize a calendar, track a habit, grow a follower count, or A/B test a profile. But none of that answers whether you are loved, whether your work matters, whether your suffering has been integrated, or whether your life has coherence.

A car can be solved mechanically. A marriage cannot. A calling cannot. A soul cannot. Living things are not puzzles that become complete once you find the correct technique.

## Meaning Has Three Ingredients

Brooks frames meaning as involving:

1. **Coherence** — your life has a story, shape, and intelligible pattern.
2. **Purpose** — you are pointed toward something that cannot be exhausted by a single completed goal.
3. **Significance** — you matter beyond being noticed; you are loved, needed, and embedded in something larger than yourself.

This is why fame is such a poor substitute for significance. Fame proves attention. It does not prove love. Approval proves reaction. It does not prove belonging.

## The Achievement Trap

Ambitious people are vulnerable because achievement offers a clean scoreboard. When life feels uncertain, metrics feel mercifully concrete.

But a scoreboard can become a way to avoid the actual question: *what is this for?*

This connects to [Frankl's Inverse Law - Meaning vs Pleasure](/wisdom/personal-growth/presence-and-happiness/frankls-inverse-law-meaning-vs-pleasure) and [Presence Time and Busyness](/wisdom/personal-growth/presence-and-happiness/presence-time-and-busyness). A person can keep postponing life into a future where achievement will finally authorize joy, only to discover that the postponement itself became the life.

It also connects to [Arrival Fallacy](/wisdom/personal-growth/presence-and-happiness/arrival-fallacy): completed goals rarely deliver the permanent inner state people imagined. The destination quickly becomes the next starting line.

## Return to Reality

The antidote is not to reject technology or ambition. It is to stop asking simulations to do the work of reality.

Meaning tends to return through contact with things that cannot be fully optimized:

- face-to-face love
- silence and boredom
- beauty
- service
- commitment
- embodied presence
- real friendship
- chosen responsibility
- suffering metabolized into compassion or courage
- work that would still matter without applause

If a life feels unreal, more stimulation is usually the wrong medicine. The better question is: where have I replaced contact with proxies?

## Practical Questions

- What am I measuring because I am afraid to ask what matters?
- Where am I seeking approval because significance feels harder to earn?
- Which simulated reward leaves me emptier after it ends?
- What real-world contact am I avoiding through technology, work, or optimization?
- What would still feel meaningful if nobody could see it?

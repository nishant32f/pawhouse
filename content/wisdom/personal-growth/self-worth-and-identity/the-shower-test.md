# The Shower Test

Your real priorities are often revealed in the first quiet place where nobody is trying to sell you a desire.

Jimmy Carr's heuristic is simple:

> "You can tell what you care about by what you think about in the shower."

The point is not that every stray thought is profound. The point is that attention becomes more honest when external inputs disappear. In the shower, on a walk, or in any other liminal space without a feed, inbox, podcast, or task list, the mind drifts toward the unresolved thing it actually cares about.

## What Quiet Reveals

The shower test separates declared priorities from lived priorities.

You may say you care about a goal, relationship, craft, or problem. But when the world goes quiet, the mind keeps voting. It returns to the subject with emotional charge: the thing being avoided, built, grieved, envied, protected, or secretly chosen.

This links to [[Presence Time and Busyness]]: busyness can become an anesthetic. Remove the noise and the avoided signal comes back.

## Borrowed Wants

The test is also a filter against [[Mimetic Desire]]. Many ambitions are copied from the status game nearby: money because someone else has it, a relationship as a social proof object, a career path because it photographs well.

Carr's sharper question is not just "what do you want?" It is: whose desire is this?

If the desire only survives in public, it may be status theatre. If it keeps appearing in solitude, it is more likely to be genuinely yours.

## Choosing Means Excluding

An interesting life is exclusive. To choose one life is to close off other plausible lives.

This is why the first adventure is deciding what you want. The second is earning it. Most people try to skip the first by borrowing desires, then wonder why the work feels hollow.

The practical move is to notice what you already return to unprompted, then ask whether you are willing to accept the whole package: the craft, repetitions, awkward apprenticeship, opportunity cost, and boring logistics.

## Want The Lifestyle, Not Just The Life

The outcome is never sold separately from the process.

If you want to be a comedian, the package includes testing jokes, bombing, touring, writing, revising, and developing the lens that turns unrelated inputs into material. If you want to be a musician, the package may include years of practice before the visible part begins.

This is the same warning as [[Stop Wanting Something You're Not Willing to Work For]]: wanting the result while rejecting the lifestyle guarantees disappointment.

## Practical Rule

Make quiet time diagnostic.

Ask:

- What does my mind return to when nothing is pulling on it?
- Is this desire mine, or am I imitating a nearby status game?
- Do I want the unglamorous daily lifestyle, or only the photographed outcome?
- What alternate life am I willing to close off to pursue this one properly?

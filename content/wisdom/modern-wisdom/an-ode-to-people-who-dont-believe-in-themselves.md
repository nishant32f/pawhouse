# An Ode To People Who Don't Believe In Themselves

Source: [Chris Williamson / Modern Wisdom — YouTube](https://youtu.be/yBEfU-Ul_iI)  
Duration: 6:53

## Practical takeaway:

You do not need self-belief before action. Act first, generate evidence, and let belief catch up. While doing that, do not grip the work so tightly that even success only feels like relief.

## Core ideas

- Self-belief is overrated as a prerequisite. The world mostly responds to the actions you take, not the private emotional weather underneath them.
- Introspective people with high standards often struggle to “fake it until you make it.” A better route is to make it until you believe it.
- Chronic seriousness creates brittle fragility. Playfulness is not unseriousness; it is protection for long-term persistence.
- Winning should feel like joy, not merely the removal of fear.
- Emotional pain is information and depth, not proof that you are failing.
- Your goals and attachments are hypotheses to be tested, not ideologies to be defended.
- After basic material comfort, the work is ultimately for your own conscience.

## Clean transcript

**0:00** An ode to people who don't believe in themselves.

What comes first, belief or action? Do you need to believe that you can do a thing before you can do it?

“Fake it until you make it” is one option, but it is incredibly hard if you are introspective and have low self-belief and high standards. So what about: make it until you believe it?

Here are some lessons I have learned.

You can believe you are not worthy of a thing and still attain it. You can be adamant that your efforts are going to go badly and still succeed. You can grip and grasp and fear and ruin the enjoyment, and be totally unwarranted, and things still go well.

You can have no self-belief and show up anyway. You can want more for yourself without knowing exactly what that looks like. You can doubt the process, question your talent, be uncertain that you are making progress, disparage your accomplishments, permanently feel like you are not working hard enough no matter how hard you work, never give yourself a break, fail to fully feel gratitude, be terrified of never reaching your goals, and still end up in a place that your 20-year-old self could not imagine.

You get to.

**1:01** Self-belief is overrated. Generate evidence, says Ryan Holiday.

This resilience of doing good things in spite of you not believing in them, or believing that they are going to happen, is pretty miraculous.

I am questioning whether or not it is virtuous to do something virtuous if you do not feel like you had any other choice. Ultimately, you can get to a place that you would be very surprised by regardless of how motivated you feel to get there.

The world really only knows the actions that you take. It does not know about the internal state until that shows up in the way that you actually perform.

So, some things to keep in mind.

### 1. Do not grip life so tightly

**1:53** Being too serious creates a kind of brittle fragility which a playful attitude insulates you against.

Your goal is dynamic persistence over the long term. Taking things seriously gives you a huge advantage in bursts, but chronic seriousness makes you rigid and at risk of blow-up.

A question from Joe Hudson from the start of the year was: what would this be like if it was 10% more enjoyable?

When you are sat in a meeting, when you are having a conversation with your partner, when you are playing sport, when you are doing something that you love, or something that you hate, or something that is in between: what would this be like if it was 10% more enjoyable?

Matthew McConaughey said: make a sense of humor your default emotion.

### 2. Do not forget what winning is supposed to feel like

**2:40** Do not be so worried about winning that you forget what winning is supposed to feel like.

Is your presiding feeling when things go well one of happiness and satisfaction, or one of relief? Is it joy, or simply the abatement of fear?

After a while of winning, you realize that how you win is more important than if you win. How you feel during the event is more important than the outcome of the event. How the people who read your work are impacted is more important than how many people are impacted.

You cannot be so terrified of failing that even the act of winning is made miserable.

### 3. It is all vibes

**3:20** Ultimately, you are doing things not to say that you have done them, but for the experience of having done them.

When you look back, it is the experience itself, and not just the outcome, that matters. Sure, outcomes are more important than inputs, but vibes are more important than all of that still, because that is what you are going to remember: your felt experience of it.

One of the big determining factors in how you feel will be the outcome. I am not saying that you need to forget about winning, forget about your desires and your goals, and just enjoy the moment under a tree [unclear]. You still need to go and do the things.

But do not underrate how important the actual process is, and making sure that you enjoy it. Oddly enough, optimizing for how you feel detaches you from caring about the outcomes, but is the very thing that will drive outcomes the most.

And if it does not, what do you care? You are enjoying it.

### 4. Emotional pain is a hell of a teacher, but it will not kill you

**4:16** Would life be easier if you did not feel everything so very deeply? Perhaps.

But the only reason you are getting the outcomes that you want is because of your depth of thought. As bad as it feels, this that you are going through right now is the breadth of human experience. You are alive. Your inner landscape is a fascinating world to explore, so act with curiosity.

What you are doing right now — your goals, the desire for attainment, your attachments to this world — are hypotheses to be tested, not ideologies to be proved.

### 5. Is your goal to survive, thrive, or flourish?

**5:03** You have probably dealt with everything that life has thrown at you so far.

Do you think that is because of the way that you grasped and controlled and feared and ruminated? Or could it be because you are a capable, competent, gifted person, and the world is fundamentally fair, and over a long enough time horizon most people get what they deserve?

### 6. You are doing this for you

**5:30** This is something I have only realized relatively recently: after a certain level of material comfort, the only person that you need to do this thing for is you.

Your conscience knows when you are being honest and when you are not. I think optimizing to make that person happy — that conscience happy — is a really good idea.

You should basically be the person that your mom thinks you are, or the person that your youngest self wanted as a role model: brave, courageous, earnest, honest, virtuous, like on fire.

There is this quote from the ancient Greeks that says: live as though all your ancestors were living again through you. I really love that.

We judge people based on how much life they can tolerate, how much feeling they can deal with and carry and enjoy. That is something that we should all try to have more of in ourselves: being the person that your younger self wanted as a role model, being the person that your mom thinks you are. I think that is a good rubric.

# Business Scaling Principles

### The Scaling Journey

```
┌─────────────┐      ┌─────────────┐      ┌─────────────┐      ┌─────────────┐
│  FDE STAGE  │ ───> │   TOOLING   │ ───> │ GENERALIZE  │ ───> │  PLATFORM   │
│ (#1, #4)    │      │  (#2, #6)   │      │  (#3, #8)   │      │ (#10, #12)  │
└─────────────┘      └─────────────┘      └─────────────┘      └─────────────┘
      │                    │                    │                    │
 Customize          Build primitives      Extract reusable     Land→Expand→
 at scale          for FDE leverage        concepts            Abstract
 Risk-weighted      Demo-driven           Validate across      Platformize
 deals              workflows             customers            pattern
```

**Cross-cutting**: #5 (Exec top-5), #7 (Pain = signal), #9 (Avoid local maxima), #11 (Hire rebels)

1. **FDE = "do things that don't scale—at scale."** The goal isn't to reduce customization per customer; it's to keep customization while increasing outcome value and contract size.

2. **Your second user is the FDE.** Build product primitives and tooling that increase FDE leverage every quarter; if field teams don't prefer your abstractions, you built the wrong thing.

3. **Generalize one level higher than the request.** Never productize a site-specific workflow; extract the reusable concept (ontology/primitives), validated across multiple customers before shipping.

4. **Sell outcomes, not installs.** Early deals can be risk-weighted ("pay when it works"); as outcomes expand, contracts must stair-step up—complex, flexible, but value aligned.

5. **Executive top-5 or bust.** If your wedge isn't on the CEO's priority list, IT/process gravity wins. Executive air cover is a technical dependency, not a sales nicety.

6. **Demo-driven ≠ cheap theatrics.** A great demo is a forcing function: every new feature must earn its place in a believable end-to-end workflow that creates customer desire, not just agreement.

7. **Pain is the signal.** Rewrites, rough prototypes, and friction between field and product can easily be "process'd" away. If it's painless, you're probably not discovering.

8. **Measure the right two things:** (a) Outcome value delivered (even if you can't capture it all yet), and (b) Product leverage (field effort per unit value should drop across customers).

9. **Beware "sponsor local maxima."** Mid-level asks often optimize convenience, not impact. Re-anchor on enterprise outcomes; otherwise you drift into services.

10. **Why this works for AI now:** There's no incumbent product for agents and adoption lags capability—so discovery has to happen inside the customer, repeatedly, with platformization trailing behind.

11. **Hire rebels, not natives.** Echo roles need domain insiders who think the current way is insufficient; deltas are rapid iteration, not long-horizon craftsspeople.

12. **Land → expand → abstract.** Win with one compelling outcome, use proximity to find bigger adjacent problems, then fold the pattern back into the platform. Repeat.

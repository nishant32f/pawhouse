# Unslop

Use this skill after selecting the content and writing a first draft. Its job is to edit the prose, not to choose new stories or add claims.

## Preserve the evidence

Keep every selected fact, number, name, date, qualification, quote, identifier, and source URL intact in meaning. A company claim stays attributed. A small test stays a small test. Preserve quoted text exactly, including its punctuation. Do not edit code, link targets, or frontmatter as a style correction.

If the draft contains a factual error, return to the source and record the correction separately. Do not conceal an unsupported claim by making it sound simpler or more confident.

## Edit the prose

1. Start with what happened. Remove "the important shift", "the broader pattern", "at its core", and similar introductions when they replace a fact.
2. Replace abstract significance claims with concrete actions, properties, or measured results. Delete claims of importance that add no information.
3. Name the actor. Prefer "the developer tested" over "tests were conducted" when the source names the developer.
4. Use plain words. Replace "leverage" with "use", "facilitate" with "help", and vague "infrastructure primitives" with the actual computer, API, database, or tool.
5. Split sentences that need rereading. Aim for 25 words or fewer, while retaining necessary conditions and exact quotations.
6. Remove forced contrasts such as "not merely X, but Y", repeated three-item rhythms, promotional adjectives, dramatic fragments, and recap endings.
7. Remove repeated advice, decorative bold labels, title-case headings, and process commentary. Format only where it helps scanning.
8. Use periods and commas instead of em dashes in authored prose. Use straight quotation marks. Preserve source quotations exactly.
9. Keep the same term for the same thing. Do not rename it across paragraphs to create variety.
10. Keep a sentence that already passes. Do not replace one kind of stock phrase with another.

## Read back

Read the edited text once without the draft. Each sentence should give the reader a specific fact or useful consequence. Then compare it with the draft and original evidence. Check numbers, names, qualifiers, quotations, and URLs.

For a digest, keep the first draft and edited copy private until validation. Save concrete before/after examples and source checks in the run's private review. If the draft needs no edits, say so without inventing an editing history. Do not put the review or an "unslopped" badge into the published prose.
